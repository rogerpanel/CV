\begin{abstract}
Learned network intrusion detectors are increasingly deployed in cloud and
enterprise networks, yet an attacker who controls part of the traffic can shift
the flow statistics they rely on. We introduce SODE-Guard, an intrusion detector
built on a neural stochastic differential equation (SDE), together with a
training and certification recipe and an evaluation protocol designed for
adversarial settings. The recipe combines imbalance-aware training with a
post-hoc logit adjustment, adversarial training whose adversary differentiates
through the deployed multi-path predictor with fresh Brownian paths, and a
randomized-smoothing certificate that treats Gaussian input noise as a
pure-diffusion prefix of the SDE. We prove that this certificate is sound for a
stochastic base classifier and retain a deterministic Lipschitz certificate for
the discretized dynamics. We evaluate on four corpora from different networks
and feature extractors (Kubernetes container flows, UNSW-NB15,
CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and CIC-IoT-2023) with deduplication, five seeds, eight adaptive attacks,
feature-constrained attacks, conformal alarm thresholds and pre-declared
statistical contrasts. On every corpus the recipe significantly raises clean
macro-F1, by 0.016 to 0.144, and worst-case macro-F1, by 0.129 to 0.174.
SODE-Guard is level with or ahead of matched adversarially trained baselines and
certifies within 0.02 of the best smoothed baseline, whereas gradient-boosted
trees, the most accurate clean models, fall to 0.02 to 0.17 under attack. A case
study shows how one leaked metadata column reversed a certification comparison.
SODE-Guard and its open protocol give practitioners robust, certifiable
detectors with controllable false-alarm rates, and give researchers a
reproducible benchmark for robust intrusion detection.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data and code availability}
The container flows are part of the ICS3D collection \citep{anaedevha2025ics3d}
and the UNSW-NB15, CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and CIC-IoT-2023 flows are taken from the IIS3D collection
\citep{anaedevha2025iis3d}; both are public on Kaggle, and the original corpora are
cited in \cref{sec:exp}. The code, the resumable multi-seed driver, a Colab
notebook, and the per-seed result and prediction files behind every table and
figure are available at \coderepo.

\section*{Ethical considerations}
This work is defensive and aims to make intrusion detectors harder to evade. All
attacks are standard and public, and the data contain flow statistics only, with
no payloads or personal identifiers. Certificates cover the stated threat model;
a detector should be one layer of a defence in depth.
