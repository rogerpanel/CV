Update after the five-seed re-run (see also the general update)

Thank you again for the constructive review. Here is how the update answers each point.

Q1 (paths during PGD). Every attack is scored under both settings:
- fixed Brownian paths (seeds 0-7, a deterministic target, attacked exactly by PGD with restarts);
- fresh paths on every call (EOT-PGD, APGD with EOT, and a 64-path expected-margin attack).

The two agree closely. Worst-case macro-F1 for SODE-Guard with EOT-matched adversarial training is 0.420 with fixed paths and 0.412 with fresh paths.

W5 (adaptive attacks). Adversarial training through the deployed 8-path predictor, with fresh paths in every step (module 2), raises worst-case macro-F1 from 0.245 to 0.420 ± 0.010 over five seeds (Holm-adjusted p = 0.016). That is on par with an adversarially trained MLP (0.424) and neural ODE (0.432); the differences are not significant. The masking checks pass. A full-strength re-check of seed 0 (PGD-200 with 5 restarts, EOT-32, a 256-path expected-margin attack, Square with 5,000 queries) is running now.

W4 (certification). SODE-Guard is now certified with randomised smoothing over the input and its own Brownian paths. Certified accuracy at l2 radius 0.273 is 0.556 ± 0.033, against 0.096 with Theorem A before. That is on par with a smoothed MLP (0.555). Reweighted to the test class mix, it is 0.843.

W3 (recent and strong baselines). We added:
- LightGBM, XGBoost and a random forest;
- adversarially trained MLP and neural ODE, with the same recipe as SODE-Guard;
- a smoothed MLP;
- a neural ODE with the same imbalance-aware training.

The trees reach the highest clean macro-F1 (0.61-0.62) but fall to 0.02-0.04 under a black-box Square attack, while adversarially trained neural models keep about 0.42. The revision discusses BARS (NDSS 2023) and MARS (2025) as certified traffic classifiers with anisotropic smoothing, a natural extension of module 3.

W1, W2 (motivation, figures). The robustness gap of the strongest clean models gives the revision a concrete motivation:
- trees are best on clean flows and weakest under attack;
- a robust neural detector closes that gap.

Figures 1-2 will be redrawn around the three modules and the clean / worst-case / certified trade-off.

Running now, for the revision:

- a per-class chronological split;
- the full-strength attack check;
- also planned: feature-constrained attacks and a second container dataset.

We hope the update addresses your concerns, and would be grateful if you could consider it in your final assessment.
