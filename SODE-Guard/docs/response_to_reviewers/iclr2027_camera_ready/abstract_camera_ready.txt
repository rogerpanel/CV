Neural intrusion detectors summarise every network flow by a few dozen traffic statistics, and an attacker who controls part of the traffic can shift those statistics. We present Stochastic ODE-Guard (SODE-Guard), a neural stochastic differential equation (SDE) detector that evolves an encoded flow under learned random dynamics and classifies by averaging over paths, together with a training and certification recipe built for it. The recipe has three modules: imbalance-aware training with a post-hoc logit adjustment; adversarial training whose adversary differentiates through the deployed multi-path predictor with fresh Brownian paths; and a randomised-smoothing certificate that treats Gaussian input noise as a pure-diffusion prefix of the SDE and samples it jointly with the model's own paths. We prove that this certificate is sound for the stochastic predictor, and we keep a deterministic Lipschitz certificate for the discretised SDE. On the container-flow part of the ICS3D benchmark, deduplicated and evaluated over five seeds against eight adaptive attacks, the recipe raises clean macro-F1 from 0.438 to 0.495, worst-case macro-F1 from 0.245 to 0.420, and certified accuracy at l2 radius 0.273 to 0.576 (0.877 on the test class mix), on par with the strongest adversarially trained and smoothed baselines. Gradient-boosted trees, the most accurate clean models, fall to 0.02 under a black-box attack and to 0.17 when the attacker may change only realisable traffic statistics, a setting in which SODE-Guard keeps 0.446; conformal alarm thresholds hold its false-alarm rate at the chosen budget. On UNSW-NB15 and CSE-CIC-IDS2018, two further corpora from different networks, the gains of every module and the brittleness of the trees carry over; SODE-Guard is level with or ahead of the matched baselines under attack, and its certificate stays within 0.02 of the best smoothed baseline. SODE-Guard gives security teams a detector whose robustness is measured under adaptive attack and certified by its own randomness, and gives researchers an open, reproducible protocol for building and testing robust intrusion detectors.
