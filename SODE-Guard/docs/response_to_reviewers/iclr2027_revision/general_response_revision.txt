Revision summary: a three-module recipe makes SODE-Guard accurate on rare attacks, robust under adaptive attack and certified by its own randomness, validated over five seeds with a protocol built for security use.

We thank the reviewers and the Area Chair. Their comments shaped this revision, and the uploaded manuscript reflects all of the work carried out during the discussion.

What is new in the method
(1) Imbalance-aware training: a logit-adjusted loss, a full training budget with early stopping on validation macro-F1, and a post-hoc logit adjustment tuned on a held-out selection split.
(2) EOT-matched adversarial training: the training adversary attacks exactly the deployed eight-path predictor, with fresh Brownian paths at every step (N-FGSM, eps ramp, early stopping on robust validation macro-F1).
(3) A smoothing certificate that uses the model's own noise. Proposition 1 proves that randomised smoothing remains sound when the base classifier is itself random. Gaussian input noise is a pure-diffusion prefix of the SDE, so SODE-Guard is certified by sampling input noise and Brownian paths jointly. Theorem A remains as a deterministic Lipschitz certificate for the discretised SDE.

How it is evaluated
- Data: container flows of ICS3D, with duplicates and conflicting labels removed before splitting.
- Seeds and splits: five seeds, each with a new 60/20/20 split.
- Attacks: eight adaptive attacks (PGD with restarts, APGD-CE/DLR with EOT, EOT-PGD, an expected-margin attack, Square, transfer), each scored with fixed and with fresh Brownian paths.
- Checks: gradient-masking checks, a full-strength re-attack, and a per-class chronological split.
- Baselines: LightGBM, XGBoost and a random forest.
- Statistics: paired-bootstrap contrasts with Holm correction, declared before the runs.

Main results (mean over five seeds)
- Clean macro-F1: 0.438 -> 0.495 with module 1 (+0.057, Holm-adjusted p = 0.016).
- Worst-case macro-F1 over all attacks: 0.245 -> 0.420 with module 2 (+0.174, Holm-adjusted p = 0.016). Fixed and fresh paths agree (0.420 vs 0.412).
- Certified accuracy at l2 radius 0.273: 0.576 with module 3 (0.877 on the test class mix), and 0.491 at radius 0.5. The deterministic certificate gave 0.096 at radius 0.273.
- SODE-Guard is on par with the strongest adversarially trained and smoothed baselines (MLP and neural ODE trained with the same recipe).
- Gradient-boosted trees reach the best clean macro-F1 (0.62) but fall to 0.02-0.04 under a black-box attack, which shows where robust neural detectors are needed.
- The full-strength re-attack and the chronological split confirm every conclusion.

How the revision answers each review
- Reviewer vjJK:
  - Adaptive attacks are central to the evaluation, and the masking checks pass.
  - The released pipeline attacks and certifies exactly the predictor that is deployed.
  - The anti-concentration regulariser is replaced by three modules, each with a significant measured effect.
- Reviewer FiAv:
  - The certificate is applied to trained detectors and is competitive with randomised smoothing of an MLP.
  - The stochastic model's distinctive strength is now explicit: its own paths are part of the smoothing distribution.
  - The protocol follows Arp et al. and TESSERACT: deduplication, multiple seeds, chronological evaluation, strong tree baselines.
  - The paper cites and discusses Liu et al. (CVPR 2020).
- Reviewer kdck:
  - Every attack is scored with fixed and with fresh paths.
  - Recent robust and certified baselines are included, and BARS and MARS are discussed.
  - Figures 1-2 are redrawn around the method and the accuracy / robustness / certification trade-off.
  - The motivation is now grounded in the measured brittleness of the most accurate clean models.

Ablations
Every module was ablated over five seeds:
- the post-hoc adjustment and the training budget account for the clean gain;
- single-step N-FGSM matches five-step PGD at lower cost;
- the consistency weight trades certified accuracy at small radii against large radii.

Future work
We will extend SODE-Guard along the lines the reviewers opened:
- flow-sequence context for the rarest attack classes;
- feature-constrained and packet-level attacks;
- anisotropic noise in the certificate, in the spirit of BARS and MARS;
- a second container corpus with cross-dataset transfer;
- conformal alarm thresholds on the held-out calibration split.

The anonymised repository contains the code, the resumable multi-seed driver, a Colab notebook and every per-seed result file behind the tables and figures.

SODE-Guard and its open protocol give practitioners a ready path to intrusion detectors that are robust by measurement and certified by construction, and give researchers a reproducible benchmark for the next generation of robust detectors.
