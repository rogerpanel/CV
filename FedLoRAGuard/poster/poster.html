FedLoRAGuard

Code & benchmark

1The problem

  • LoRA is the dominant way to fine-tune LLMs, and public hubs now host tens of thousands of shared adapters.
  • Backdoored adapters merge silently into task adapters and keep their benign utility; a cheap LoRA fine-tune can strip a chat model's safety alignment.
  • Centralised detectors (e.g. PEFTGuard) need marketplaces to hand over raw weights. That conflicts with proprietary practice, creates a single point of trust, and misses cross-marketplace lineage.

2Our idea

Model the whole adapter ecosystem as a dynamic graph, learn a detector federatedly under differential privacy, and certify how many colluding marketplaces it can withstand.

No marketplace ever shares raw adapter weights; only clipped, noised, securely aggregated updates leave a client.

4Training round

  • Server Poisson-samples marketplaces at rate q = 0.2 (10 of N = 50).
  • Each client computes its update, clips the whole update to S = 1.0 and adds one Gaussian draw (σ = 1.1).
  • Updates travel through secure aggregation; FLTrust weights each by ReLU(cos) to a vetted 200-adapter root set.
  • An Opacus PRV accountant tracks the cumulative privacy budget: εT ≈ 13.6 at T = 100.
  • Measured cost of DP + SecAgg: ≈4% extra wall-clock per round vs. non-private FedAvg.

5Theory

Thm 1 (client-update sensitivity). With GELU (ρ≈1.13) and relation weights ‖WR‖ ≤ BW:

Δ₂ ≤ min( 2S , 2S·(ρ BW dmax)L·|R|½ )

Thm 2 (certified poisoning radius). Under a bounded-update attack, the verdict on an adapter is invariant w.p. ≥ 1−Tδ for any coalition of size

k ≤ k* = ⌊ N(Φ⁻¹(p̂₁) − Φ⁻¹(p̂₂)) / (2eεr) ⌋

With N = 50, p̂₁−p̂₂ ≈ 0.6, εr = 0.5: k* = 25. The certificate uses the per-query smoothing budget εr, not the cumulative εT.

Certified bound (50%, bounded-update threat) and empirical label-flip tolerance (ρ ≤ 0.33) are distinct results.

6Mechanism: the spectral signature

Backdoored adapters concentrate trigger information in one direction: the top singular value of BA is 2.43× larger than for benign adapters, while trailing values are indistinguishable (KS test D = 0.71, p < 10⁻³). The weight modality extracts this; the graph propagates it along lineage.

A spectral-only (SVD) baseline reaches 90.2% on simple rank-1 backdoors but only 71.4% on composite, weight-poisoning and trigger-free merging attacks, and gives no certificate.

9Robustness

  • Stricter splits: lineage-disjoint 95.1 · base-model-disjoint 93.7 · attack-family held-out 92.3 (vs. 96.4 random split).
  • Missing lineage edges: 95.6 / 93.8 / 91.1 at 10 / 30 / 50% removed.
  • Stronger privacy: 94.1% macro-F1 at εT = 1.0.
  • Byzantine label-flip: no certificate loss up to ρ ≤ 0.33.
  • Transfer: same backbone reaches 95.8% macro-F1 on 4 network-IDS datasets.

10Take-aways

  • Adapter integrity can be verified across marketplaces without sharing weights, within 1.7 pp of a centralised detector.
  • Lineage over time is the key signal beyond each adapter's own weights.
  • DP turns into a verifiable guarantee: a certified bound on colluding marketplaces.

11Scope & limits

  • Certificate covers bounded-update adversaries, not arbitrary Byzantine gradients.
  • Lineage edges are partly synthesised from HuggingGraph and PADBench.
  • 50/50 benchmark split is a design choice; deploy with a low-FPR threshold and human review.
