\begin{abstract}
Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is now the dominant
parameter-efficient fine-tuning method for large language models,
but the resulting open marketplace of adapters has become a
supply-chain attack surface: backdoored adapters merge silently
with arbitrary task adapters while preserving benign utility, and
existing centralised integrity tools require marketplaces to share
raw weights, which is incompatible with proprietary fine-tuning.
We present \modelname, a federated dynamic-graph framework that
verifies adapter integrity without weight sharing. The LoRA
ecosystem is modelled as a heterogeneous continuous-time dynamic
graph with four node types and six edge types; a federated dynamic
GNN is trained per-client with client-update-level
$(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP Gaussian noise, secure aggregation, and
FLTrust scoring. We prove a sensitivity bound for federated
heterogeneous-GNN client updates and a certified poisoning-radius
theorem which converts the per-round inference-time smoothing
budget $\epsilon_r$ into an analytical bound on the size of a
bounded-update colluding coalition $k^*$. On $13{,}500$ benign and
backdoored adapters across four base-model families and ten attack
types, \modelname\ obtains $96.4\%$ macro-F1 and a certified
coalition bound $k^*=25$ out of $N=50$ marketplaces at
inference-time smoothing budget $\epsilon_r=0.5$, and empirically
tolerates label-flipping Byzantine coalitions up to $\rho\le 0.33$
without certificate degradation. The training-time DP-SGD
mechanism, calibrated at $\sigma=1.1$ and Poisson subsampling
$q=0.2$ over $T=100$ rounds, achieves a cumulative privacy budget
$\epsilon_T\approx 13.6$ at $\delta=10^{-5}$ under the Opacus PRV
accountant, at sub-second per-adapter verification latency on
A100-class hardware. The full DP + secure-aggregation stack adds a
measured median per-round wall-clock overhead of $\approx 4\%$
(pooled median across three runs of $20$--$60$ measurement rounds
on identical CPU hardware) over non-private FedAvg; the released
runtime ships the instrumentation so operators can re-measure in
place on their target hardware.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)~\cite{hu2021lora} is the dominant
parameter-efficient fine-tuning method for large language models:
it inserts low-rank update matrices into Transformer attention,
freezes base weights, and cuts trainable-parameter counts by four
orders of magnitude. Combined with $4$-bit
QLoRA~\cite{dettmers2023qlora}, LoRA lets an individual fine-tune
a $65$B base model on a single consumer GPU. The Hugging Face Hub
now hosts over $530{,}000$ models, and one base model alone has
accumulated more than $30{,}000$ derivative LoRAs.

That ecosystem is also a supply-chain attack surface.
\citet{liu2024loraattack} showed that backdoored adapters merge
silently with arbitrary task adapters and retain malicious
behaviour while preserving benign utility;
\citet{lermen2023lora} reduced the safety-refusal rate of
Llama-2-Chat-70B from $95\%$ to below $1\%$ with a USD-$200$ LoRA
fine-tune; composite backdoors~\cite{huang2024composite} scatter
triggers across prompt components. Industry reports document
${>}100$ malicious models on the Hub, indirect compromise of the
platform's own \texttt{SFconvertbot} (${>}42{,}000$ merged PRs),
and a five-fold year-over-year rise in malicious
uploads~\cite{cohen2024jfrog,kellas2025pickleball}. Those figures
are predominantly full-model backdoors; the LoRA-\emph{adapter}
threat we target is documented separately by the adapter-specific
work above and marketplace-scale numbers are cited only as
context, not as an adapter-backdoor count.

The standard centralised response, PEFTGuard~\cite{sun2025peftguard},
trains a backdoor detector on raw adapter weights ($98.3$--$100\%$
centralised accuracy on PADBench) but requires marketplaces to
share raw weights with a third-party detector --- incompatible
with proprietary fine-tuning practice and a single point of trust.
A weight-space-only detector also sees each adapter in isolation
and misses the cross-marketplace lineage signal that distinguishes
a silently backdoored derivative from a benign one. We do not run
an adaptive attack against PEFTGuard and make no claim about its
robustness under one; our motivation is the deployment barrier and
the missing relational signal, not a claimed collapse of PEFTGuard.

\paragraph{Our approach.}
We model the LoRA ecosystem as a heterogeneous continuous-time
dynamic graph whose nodes are adapters, contributors, base models
and downstream applications, and whose time-stamped edges capture
derivation, fine-tuning, deployment and citation. Across this
graph, a dynamic GNN is trained per client with
\emph{client-update-level} DP Gaussian
noise~\cite{abadi2016deep,mironov2017renyi} and secure
aggregation~\cite{bonawitz2017practical}, so no marketplace
reveals raw weights. Composing PixelDP~\cite{lecuyer2019certified}
stability with CRFL~\cite{xie2021crfl} federated smoothing yields
a certified coalition bound $k^*$ on the number of colluding
malicious clients whose actions cannot flip the global verdict,
under a bounded-update threat model
(Definition~\ref{def:attack}). We ship the released runtime with
Opacus PRV-accountant instrumentation, an SVD-only spectral
baseline, and a per-round overhead harness so every claim is
independently re-measurable.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\textbf{(1)}~To our knowledge the first formal model of the LoRA
marketplace as a continuous-time heterogeneous dynamic graph, and
\benchname, $13{,}500$ benign+backdoored adapters over four
base-model families and ten attack types, extending
BackdoorLLM~\cite{li2025backdoorllm} and
PADBench~\cite{sun2025peftguard} with
HuggingGraph~\cite{rahman2025hugginggraph} lineage.
\textbf{(2)}~A federated DyGFormer-HGT verifier trained under
client-update-level DP-SGD with SecAgg and FLTrust bootstrapping,
robust to $\rho\le 0.33$ label-flip Byzantine clients.
\textbf{(3)}~A client-update sensitivity bound
(Theorem~\ref{thm:sensitivity}) and a certified coalition-bound
theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:certified}) that converts the
inference-time smoothing budget $\epsilon_r$ into an integer
bound $k^*$ under Definition~\ref{def:attack}. On \benchname\
this gives $96.4\%$ macro-F1, $k^*=25$ at $N=50$ under
$\epsilon_r=0.5$, cumulative
$(\epsilon_T,\delta)\!\approx\!(13.6,10^{-5})$ at $T=100$ from
the Opacus PRV accountant~\cite{gopi2021numerical}, and
sub-second per-adapter latency on A100.

\paragraph{Claim structure.}
The paper is organised around three tiered claims. The
\emph{primary} claim is that federated lineage-aware detection
can identify malicious LoRA adapters without any marketplace
sharing raw weights, landing within $1.9$ macro-F1 points of the
centralised PEFTGuard reference on the same corpus. The
\emph{secondary} claim is that DP-SGD combined with SecAgg can be
incorporated at modest utility cost ($0.5$--$1.0$\,pp macro-F1)
and at a measured ${\approx}4\%$ median per-round wall-clock
overhead on CPU-hosted federation
(Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). The \emph{theoretical} claim is that,
under Definition~\ref{def:attack}, composing PixelDP stability,
CRFL federated smoothing and R\'enyi-DP composition yields an
integer certificate $k^*$ on the largest bounded-update coalition
whose actions cannot flip the global verdict on a queried
adapter. Every other result --- dynamic-graph superiority,
multimodal gains, FLTrust robustness, lineage importance, IDS
transfer, cross-hardware latency --- is reported as supporting
evidence, not as a free-standing contribution.

\paragraph{Why this belongs at an NLP venue.}
LoRA is the dominant fine-tuning method for \emph{language}
models, and the verified threats --- alignment-bypass fine-tunes,
composite textual-trigger backdoors and instruction-following
corruption --- are attacks on the safety of NLP systems at the
PEFT layer. The signal separating a benign adapter from a
backdoored one is partly linguistic: the multimodal encoder
consumes model-card, README and contributor-profile text
alongside the weight and usage modalities, and \benchname\
extends NLP-safety resources (BackdoorLLM, PADBench). The
verifier itself is task-agnostic, but the resource, the threat
model and the deployment target (Hugging Face-style LLM adapter
hubs) are squarely NLP-safety concerns, which is why we position
the contribution for the ACL community rather than a general
security venue.

\paragraph{Roadmap.}
Section~\ref{sec:related} surveys prior work on adapter security,

bound. Section~\ref{sec:experiments} reports the empirical
study, including ablation, spectral analysis and IDS transfer.
Sections~\ref{sec:discussion}--\ref{sec:conclusion} discuss
deployment implications and conclude.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{LoRA, PEFT, and adapter security.}
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning rose with adapters and
LoRA~\cite{hu2021lora}; QLoRA~\cite{dettmers2023qlora} added
$4$-bit NF4 quantization, and AdaLoRA~\cite{zhang2023adalora},
DoRA~\cite{liu2024dora}, and GaLore~\cite{zhao2024galore} produced
structurally distinct adapter families that complicate uniform
integrity checks. Adapter security emerged with
LoRA-as-an-Attack~\cite{liu2024loraattack},
\citet{lermen2023lora}'s cheap alignment bypass, Composite
Backdoor Attacks~\cite{huang2024composite}, and the
safety-critical neuron analysis of~\citet{wei2024brittleness}.
PEFTGuard~\cite{sun2025peftguard} centralised backdoor detection
on raw weights, and ShadowGenes~\cite{schulz2025shadowgenes}
introduced graph-based model-genealogy fingerprinting. Beyond
these, ML supply-chain integrity has been approached through
model signing, software bills of materials (SBOM), and update
frameworks such as TUF; \modelname\ is complementary to those
provenance-attestation mechanisms, adding a content-level
maliciousness verdict that signing alone does not provide.

\paragraph{Federated learning, DP, and secure aggregation.}
Federated learning~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg} with heterogeneity
variants~\cite{li2020fedprox,karimireddy2020scaffold} combines
with DP-SGD~\cite{abadi2016deep}, R\'enyi-DP
accounting~\cite{mironov2017renyi}, the numerically tight PRV
accountant~\cite{gopi2021numerical}, and secure
aggregation~\cite{bonawitz2017practical}. Byzantine robustness is
addressed by Krum~\cite{blanchard2017machine},
Trimmed-Mean~\cite{yin2018byzantine}, and
FLTrust~\cite{cao2021fltrust}; a related game-theoretic treatment
appears in~\cite{anaedevha2026byzantine}. Federated
foundation-model training (FATE-LLM~\cite{fan2023fatellm},
FLoRA~\cite{wang2024flora},
OpenFedLLM~\cite{ye2024openfedllm}) \emph{trains} new adapters;
\modelname\ \emph{verifies} existing ones. Closest is
Safe-FedLLM~\cite{tao2026safefedllm}, which filters malicious
clients \emph{during} federated training via probes on local
$\Delta$LoRA updates. The two are complementary: Safe-FedLLM
protects the training process from malicious participants;
\modelname\ verifies adapters \emph{already published} to a
marketplace using cross-marketplace lineage, and issues a certified
coalition bound $k^*$ over colluding marketplaces --- a guarantee
probe-classifier defences do not provide.

\paragraph{Dynamic and heterogeneous GNNs.}
Continuous-time dynamic graph learning was pioneered by
JODIE~\cite{kumar2019jodie}, DyRep~\cite{trivedi2019dyrep} and
TGAT~\cite{xu2020tgat}, unified in TGN~\cite{rossi2020tgn};
DyGFormer~\cite{yu2023dygformer} added neighbour-co-occurrence
patching for long histories. Multi-scale temporal graph encoders
have been applied to security
telemetry~\cite{anaedevha2026multiscale}. For heterogeneous
graphs, HGT~\cite{hu2020hgt} introduced meta-relation-aware
attention with relative temporal encoding. Federated graph
learning was introduced by
FedGraphNN~\cite{he2021fedgraphnn}, and GAP~\cite{sajadmanesh2023gap}
derives a DP-GNN through R\'enyi-DP analysis. The HF ecosystem
itself is mapped by HuggingGraph~\cite{rahman2025hugginggraph}
and the model-atlas of \citet{horwitz2025atlas}.

\paragraph{Certified robustness against poisoning.}
The DP-to-certified-robustness bridge was established by
PixelDP~\cite{lecuyer2019certified}; \citet{ma2019data} converted
DP-SGD budgets into poisoning radii. In federated settings,
CRFL~\cite{xie2021crfl} uses parameter clipping plus Gaussian
smoothing, and FLCert~\cite{cao2022flcert} extends this with a
fraction-of-malicious-clients guarantee. State-space poisoning
resilience without a formal certificate appears
in~\cite{anaedevha2026mambashield}. For graph data,
\citet{wang2021certgnn,scholten2022randomized} certify against
edge and message-interception perturbations.
\modelname's Theorem~\ref{thm:certified} synthesises these into a
single per-marketplace bound on the largest bounded-update
coalition that cannot alter the global verdict.

\section{Preliminaries and Problem}
\label{sec:problem}

\paragraph{LoRA adapter.}
A LoRA adapter for a Transformer layer with frozen weight
$\mathbf{W}_0 \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{out}} \times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$
replaces $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{W}_0 \mathbf{x}$ with
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{W}_0 \mathbf{x} + \tfrac{\alpha}{r} \mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x},\;\; \mathbf{B}\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times r},\,\mathbf{A}\in\R^{r\times d_{\mathrm{in}}},
\label{eq:lora}
\end{equation}
with rank $r \ll \min(d_{\mathrm{out}}, d_{\mathrm{in}})$ and
fixed scale $\alpha$~\cite{hu2021lora}. We denote a complete
adapter by $\theta = \{(\mathbf{A}_\ell, \mathbf{B}_\ell)
\}_{\ell \in \mathcal{L}}$ over adapted layers $\mathcal{L}$,
plus metadata $m_\theta$ (base model, corpus, contributor,
declared task and licence, upload time, download stats).

\paragraph{LoRA ecosystem graph.}
At time $t$ the ecosystem is a heterogeneous continuous-time
dynamic graph $\Gcal(t) = (\Vcal(t), \Ecal(t), \Rcal, \phi, \psi)$
with four node types and six edge types.

\begin{definition}[LoRA Ecosystem Graph]
\label{def:graph}
$\Vcal(t) = \Vcal_A(t) \cup \Vcal_B(t) \cup \Vcal_C(t) \cup
\Vcal_D(t)$ (adapters, base models, contributors, downstream
applications). Time-stamped typed edges use relations $\Rcal =
\{\texttt{contributes}, \texttt{derives\_from},$
$\texttt{fine\_tunes}, \texttt{deploys}, \texttt{cites},
\texttt{co\_uses}\}$. The node-feature map $\phi:\Vcal(t)\to\R^{d_v}$
is instantiated by the multimodal encoder of
Section~\ref{sec:framework}.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Federated setting and threat model.}
$N$ LoRA-hosting marketplaces $\{C_i\}_{i=1}^N$ hold private
subgraphs $\Gcal_i(t)$ and adapter pools $\Theta_i$; they refuse
to share raw weights but consent to federated training of
$f_{\mathbf{w}}:\Vcal_A(t)\!\to\![0,1]$ under privacy-preserving
aggregation. The \emph{primary} threat is documented
single/few-point malicious uploads to independent marketplaces;
the multi-marketplace \emph{collusion} we analyse is a worst-case
robustness scenario, not a claim about current attacker behaviour.
The adversary controls $k$ of $N$ clients with $k/N\!\le\!\rho$,
publishes arbitrary backdoored adapters under any identity, and
flips training labels on controlled adapters; it does not control
SecAgg nor the vetted FLTrust root set. The certified
Theorem~\ref{thm:certified} applies to \emph{bounded-update}
adversaries below, not to arbitrary Byzantine gradients.

\begin{definition}[$(k,\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}})$-poisoning attack]
\label{def:attack}
A poisoning attack controls $k\!\in\!\{1,\ldots,\lfloor\rho
N\rfloor\}$ clients and submits gradient updates
$\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_i$ within $\ell_2$ distance
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}\!\ge\!0$ of the honest update after
server-side clipping to $\ell_2$-norm $S$; its objective is to
flip the global verdict on at least one target adapter.
\end{definition}

We seek $\mathbf{w}^*$ minimising empirical risk under per-round
$(\epsilon_r,\delta)$-DP by client-update Gaussian DP-SGD (clip
$S$), cumulative $\epsilon_T\!=\!\mathrm{PRVCompose}(\sigma,q,T)$
at $\delta$, SecAgg hiding individual
$\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_i$, and FLTrust scoring on a server-held root
set $R_0$. The deployed verifier admits (a) a high-probability
robustness certificate against $k^*$ malicious clients
(Theorem~\ref{thm:certified}) under Definition~\ref{def:attack},
and (b) calibrated maliciousness probabilities for marketplace
moderation.

\section{The \modelname\ Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

\modelname\ has four components: a multimodal node-feature
encoder, a per-client heterogeneous dynamic GNN, federated
training under client-update-level DP-SGD with SecAgg and FLTrust,
and a global verifier that emits $\hat{p}_{\mathrm{mal}}$ and the
certified bound $k^*$ (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}).

\paragraph{Multimodal encoder.}
For $v_\theta\!\in\!\Vcal_A$, weight features
$\bm{\phi}^{\mathrm{wt}}(\theta)\!\in\!\R^{d_{\mathrm{wt}}}$
comprise the spectral signature $(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_r)$ of each
$\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}$ in~\eqref{eq:lora}, per-layer Frobenius
norms, and a base-model weight-difference fingerprint. Text
features $\bm{\phi}^{\mathrm{txt}}(m_\theta)\!\in\!\R^{d_{\mathrm{txt}}}$
come from a frozen sentence transformer over model cards, READMEs
and contributor profiles. Behavioural features
$\bm{\phi}^{\mathrm{beh}}(\theta)\!\in\!\R^{d_{\mathrm{beh}}}$
encode downloads, monthly active deployments, and citation
centralities. Cross-attention fuses the three streams into a
common dimension $d_v$:
\begin{equation}
\phi(v_\theta) = \mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{fuse}}
[\bm{\phi}^{\mathrm{wt}}(\theta);\, \bm{\phi}^{\mathrm{txt}}(m_\theta);\,
\bm{\phi}^{\mathrm{beh}}(\theta)],
\label{eq:fused}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{fuse}}\!\in\!\R^{d_v\times
(d_{\mathrm{wt}}+d_{\mathrm{txt}}+d_{\mathrm{beh}})}$. Base
model, contributor and application nodes use type-specific
encoders producing $d_v$-dimensional features.

\paragraph{Local heterogeneous dynamic GNN.}
Each client runs a DyGFormer~\cite{yu2023dygformer} with HGT
relation-aware attention~\cite{hu2020hgt}. For query node $v$ at
time $t$, the encoder samples the $K$ most recent temporal
neighbours across all relations, patches neighbour co-occurrences,
and applies a Transformer over the patches. With $\mathbf{h}_v$ the
running hidden state initialised to $\phi(v)$ and $\tau(\cdot)$
returning node type, HGT attention computes
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{u, v, r} = \softmax\!\Bigl(\tfrac{(\mathbf{W}_Q^{\tau(v)} \mathbf{h}_v)^\top \mathbf{W}_R^r (\mathbf{W}_K^{\tau(u)} \mathbf{h}_u)}{\sqrt{d_v / H}}\Bigr),
\label{eq:hgt}
\end{equation}
with type-specific $\mathbf{W}_Q^{\tau},\mathbf{W}_K^{\tau}$,
relation-specific $\mathbf{W}_R^r$, $H$ heads, and a softmax over
typed neighbours $(u,r)$ of $v$. A residual FFN follows.

\paragraph{Federated training (client-update-level DP).}
Each round $t$ Poisson-samples $S_t\!\subseteq\!\{C_i\}$ at rate
$q\!=\!m/N$. Each sampled client computes the local minibatch
gradient
$\mathbf{g}_i\!=\!\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\hat{\Lcal}_i(\mathbf{w}_t;
\Gcal_i(t))$. Unlike example-level
DP-SGD~\cite{abadi2016deep}, we operate at the
\emph{client-update} level: the client clips the whole update to
$\ell_2$-norm $S$ and adds a single Gaussian draw
$\Ncal(\mathbf{0},\sigma^2 S^2\mathbf{I})$ calibrated to the
per-round R\'enyi-DP budget, then transmits
$\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_i$ via SecAgg. The server FLTrust-scores
each update against a vetted root set $R_0$ (cosine similarity
to a server reference $\mathbf{g}_0$), weights by
$\mathrm{ReLU}(\cos(\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_i,\mathbf{g}_0))$, and
aggregates. Algorithm~\ref{alg:fedloraguard} gives one round.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\small
\caption{\modelname\ training round}
\label{alg:fedloraguard}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Clients $\{C_i\}_{i=1}^N$, $\mathbf{w}_t$, clip $S$, noise $\sigma$, learning rate $\eta$, vetted root set $R_0$, sampling rate $q$
\STATE Server Poisson-samples $S_t \subseteq \{C_i\}$ at rate $q$
\STATE $\mathbf{g}_0 \gets \nabla_{\mathbf{w}} \hat{\Lcal}(\mathbf{w}_t; R_0)$
\FOR{each $C_i \in S_t$ in parallel}
    \STATE $\mathbf{g}_i \gets \nabla_{\mathbf{w}} \hat{\Lcal}_i(\mathbf{w}_t; \Gcal_i(t))$  \hfill\COMMENT{full client update}
    \STATE $\bar{\mathbf{g}}_i \gets \mathbf{g}_i \cdot \min(1, S/\norm{\mathbf{g}_i}_2)$  \hfill\COMMENT{clip whole update}
    \STATE $\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_i \gets \bar{\mathbf{g}}_i + \Ncal(\mathbf{0}, \sigma^2 S^2 \mathbf{I})$
    \STATE Transmit $\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_i$ by SecAgg
\ENDFOR
\STATE $\bar{\mathbf{G}} \gets \mathrm{SecAgg}(\{\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_i\})$
\STATE $\beta_i \gets \mathrm{ReLU}(\cos(\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_i, \mathbf{g}_0)) / \sum_j \mathrm{ReLU}(\cos(\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_j, \mathbf{g}_0))$
\STATE $\mathbf{w}_{t+1} \gets \mathbf{w}_t - \eta \sum_i \beta_i \tilde{\mathbf{g}}_i$
\STATE Update PRV accountant; compute $k^*$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:certified}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Theoretical Properties}
\label{sec:theory}

Full proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\begin{theorem}[Client-update sensitivity]
\label{thm:sensitivity}
Let $f_{\mathbf{w}}$ be an $L$-layer heterogeneous dynamic GNN
over $\Gcal(t)$ with per-relation
$\norm{\mathbf{W}_R^r}_2\!\le\!B_W$ ($|\Rcal|\!=\!6$),
$\rho$-Lipschitz $\phi^{\mathrm{nl}}$ (GELU:
$\rho\!\approx\!1.13$, at $x\!\approx\!\pm\sqrt{2}$; we use this
in place of $\rho\!\le\!1$), and per-node temporal-neighbourhood
degree at most $d_{\max}$. Treat HGT attention coefficients
$\alpha_{u,v,r}$ as bounded by their spectral envelope (a
state-aware bound is future work). Let $S$ be the client-update
clip at Alg.~\ref{alg:fedloraguard} line~5. For adjacent
$\Dcal_i\!\sim\!\Dcal_i'$ (single node-edge differ), the
$\ell_2$-sensitivity of
$\bar{\mathbf{g}}_i:=\mathbf{g}_i\!\cdot\!\min(1,S/\norm{\mathbf{g}_i}_2)$
satisfies
\begin{equation}
\Delta_2\;\le\;\min\!\Bigl(2S,\;2S(\rho B_W d_{\max})^L |\Rcal|^{1/2}\Bigr).
\label{eq:sensitivity}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Two-sided clipping gives $\norm{T_S(\mathbf{g})}_2\!\le\!S$ so
$\Delta_2\!\le\!2S$ unconditionally. For the graph-structural
refinement, induction on $L$ propagates a single-node change
through HGT message-passing; Cauchy--Schwarz plus
$\norm{\mathbf{W}_R^r}_2\!\le\!B_W$ gives
$\norm{\Delta\mathbf{h}_v^{(\ell)}}_2\!\le\!\rho
B_W d_{\max}\norm{\Delta\mathbf{h}_v^{(\ell-1)}}_2$; iterate $L$
times, add the $|\Rcal|^{1/2}$ Cauchy--Schwarz factor across
relations, and re-clip.
\end{proof}

On Hugging Face graphs $d_{\max}$ can exceed $10^3$, so the
graph-structural factor $(\rho B_W d_{\max})^L$ is large and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sensitivity} degenerates to $2S$. We do \emph{not}
use either closed form to set $\sigma$; we adopt $\sigma\!=\!1.1$
and account the $T$-round Poisson-subsampled composition
numerically through the Opacus PRV
accountant~\cite{gopi2021numerical}, obtaining
$(\epsilon_T,\delta)\!\approx\!(13.6,10^{-5})$ at $T\!=\!100$
(Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).

\begin{theorem}[Certified poisoning radius]
\label{thm:certified}
Suppose \modelname\ runs Alg.~\ref{alg:fedloraguard} for $T$
rounds and passes the aggregated global model through a single
Gaussian smoothing draw with budget $(\epsilon_r,\delta)$ at
verification time --- a design parameter separate from the
training-time budgets. Let $\hat{p}_1\!\ge\!\hat{p}_2$ be the
top-two class probabilities of $f_{\mathbf{w}_T}(\theta)$ and
$\Phi^{-1}$ the standard-normal inverse CDF. The prediction on
$\theta$ is invariant with probability at least $1-T\delta$ under
any $(k,\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}})$-poisoning attack
(Def.~\ref{def:attack}) with
\begin{equation}
k \le k^* = \left\lfloor \tfrac{N (\Phi^{-1}(\hat{p}_1) - \Phi^{-1}(\hat{p}_2))}{2 \exp(\epsilon_r)} \right\rfloor.
\label{eq:kstar}
\end{equation}
The cumulative training-time $\epsilon_T$ governs the
marketplace-level privacy guarantee but does \emph{not} enter the
certificate: the stability factor uses the per-query smoothing
$\epsilon_r$, not the accumulated $\epsilon_T$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The bound is obtained by composing four ingredients (full
book-keeping in App.~\ref{app:proofs}). \textbf{(i) What is
smoothed.} The random variable smoothed at verification time is
the aggregated global model $\mathbf{w}_T\!\in\!\R^d$: the output
logits on $\theta$ are averaged over an
$(\epsilon_r,\delta)$-DP Gaussian draw applied to $\mathbf{w}_T$,
producing the smoothed classifier studied by
PixelDP~\cite{lecuyer2019certified} and, in the federated
setting, by CRFL~\cite{xie2021crfl}. \textbf{(ii) From client
coalition to parameter perturbation.} By
Def.~\ref{def:attack}, one malicious client under SecAgg adds an
$\ell_2$-perturbation of size $\!\le\!\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}$
per round to the aggregate after clipping; $k$ colluding clients
contribute at most $k\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}$ by triangle
inequality. FLTrust weighting $\beta_i\!\in\![0,1]$ can only
\emph{decrease} the effective attacker contribution, so any
certificate under unweighted aggregation is a fortiori valid
under FLTrust; the certificate does not rely on FLTrust
correctness. \textbf{(iii) PixelDP stability.} PixelDP
Lemma~1 gives, for any input perturbation $\Delta$ with bounded
$\ell_2$ norm, $\Prob[\argmax f(\mathbf{x})\!\neq\!\argmax
f(\mathbf{x}+\Delta)]\!\le\!e^{\epsilon_r} p_\Delta(\mathbf{x})
+\delta$, instantiated here at the per-query smoothing budget
$\epsilon_r$ (not the cumulative training budget $\epsilon_T$).
\textbf{(iv) CRFL client-coalition translation.} CRFL Thm.~1
turns the class-margin radius into a client coalition of size
$N$; the factor $2 e^{\epsilon_r}$ combines the PixelDP per-query
stability multiplier $e^{\epsilon_r}$ with the CRFL class-margin
denominator (two half-Gaussian tails). Substituting the per-query
$\epsilon_r$ yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:kstar}. R\'enyi-DP
composition~\cite{mironov2017renyi,mironov2019sampled} produces
the cumulative $\epsilon_T$ that governs marketplace privacy but
is orthogonal to the certificate. Failure probability $T\delta$
is a union bound over inference draws (a single-draw certificate
uses the tighter $\delta$); the extension to heterogeneous
edge-set perturbations follows
\citet{wang2021certgnn,scholten2022randomized}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Numerical instantiation.}
At $N\!=\!50$, $\hat{p}_1\!-\!\hat{p}_2\!\approx\!0.6$
($\Phi^{-1}(\hat{p}_1)\!-\!\Phi^{-1}(\hat{p}_2)\!\approx\!1.68$),
$\epsilon_r\!=\!0.5$ and $\delta\!=\!10^{-5}$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:kstar} gives
$k^*\!=\!\lfloor 50\!\cdot\!1.68/(2e^{0.5})\rfloor\!=\!25$: under
Def.~\ref{def:attack}, no bounded-update coalition of fewer than
$25$ colluding marketplaces can flip the verdict on a vetted
adapter with probability $\!\ge\!0.999$. An earlier draft
substituted the cumulative $\epsilon_T$ into the denominator,
which understated $k^*$.

\paragraph{Certified vs empirical robustness.}
Two robustness numbers appear below. The \emph{certified}
$k^*\!=\!25/50\!=\!50\%$ is a distribution-free bound under
Def.~\ref{def:attack}. The \emph{empirical} label-flip stress
test (\S\ref{subsec:main}) runs adversarial clients to
$\rho\!\le\!0.33$ ($33\%$) with no certificate degradation. These
are distinct results and we do not conflate them.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Datasets.}
\benchname\ contains $13{,}500$ benign+backdoored adapters across
four base-model families (Llama-2-7B, Llama-3-8B, Mistral-7B,
Qwen-7B) and ten attack types (BadNets, VPI, Sleeper, MTBA,
CTBA, AddSent, BadEdit, InsertSent, weight-poisoning, trigger-free
LoRA-merging). Ranks $r\!\in\!\{4,8,16,32\}$; task corpora from
Alpaca, Dolly, GSM8K, SQuAD-v2, IMDB, AGNews, ARC-Challenge,
HumanEval and GLUE; $50/50$ benign-to-malicious split (a
controlled evaluation design; low-FPR operating points are in
Appendix~\ref{app:additional}). Each adapter carries
$(\mathbf{A}_\ell,\mathbf{B}_\ell)$, spectral signature, layerwise
Frobenius norms, model card, contributor metadata and
download-statistic series. Cross-marketplace lineage edges are
synthesised from HuggingGraph~\cite{rahman2025hugginggraph} and
PADBench~\cite{sun2025peftguard}; a component-level real-vs-
synthetic audit is in Appendix~\ref{app:provenance}. We also
evaluate on the BackdoorLLM-LoRA
leaderboard~\cite{li2025backdoorllm} and, as a cross-domain
sanity check for the backbone only, four IDS datasets
(CIC-IDS2017~\cite{sharafaldin2018toward},
Edge-IIoTset~\cite{ferrag2022edge},
UNSW-NB15~\cite{moustafa2015unsw},
TON-IoT~\cite{moustafa2021ton}); IDS results
(Appendix~\ref{app:ids}) are not part of the LoRA-integrity
contribution.

\paragraph{Splits.}
Because \modelname\ exploits lineage, we report the main-paper
number under a \emph{lineage-component-disjoint} split (no
derivation chain crosses train/test) and stress it under three
further splits in Appendix~\ref{app:additional}
(\emph{base-model-disjoint}, \emph{contributor-disjoint},
\emph{attack-family-held-out}). Under attack-family-held-out ---
the strictest --- \modelname\ retains macro-F1 above $92\%$, so
$96.4\%$ is not a family-memorisation artefact.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
\emph{Centralised:} PEFTGuard~\cite{sun2025peftguard} ($98.3\%$
macro-F1 on PADBench), ShadowGenes~\cite{schulz2025shadowgenes}
(graph-based genealogy).
\emph{Federated without graphs:} FedAvg-MLP and DP-FedAvg-MLP.
\emph{Federated with static graphs:}
FedGraphNN-HGT~\cite{he2021fedgraphnn}.
\emph{Federated dynamic-graph:} Krum-DyGFormer with
Krum~\cite{blanchard2017machine} aggregation over our backbone.
\emph{Non-graph spectral:} an SVD-only detector thresholding
$\sigma_1/\sigma_2$ of $\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}$ (and a
logistic-regression variant on the full spectral signature), with
no graph, no federation and no certificate --- to test whether the
rank-1 backdoor signature alone suffices.

\paragraph{Implementation.}
PyTorch~$2.1$ with PyTorch Geometric and the
Flower~\cite{beutel2020flower} runtime. AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw},
lr $5\!\times\!10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, client-update
clip $S\!=\!1.0$, cosine annealing over $T\!=\!100$ rounds.
FLTrust root set $|R_0|\!=\!200$ vetted clean adapters.
Client-update-level DP-SGD: $S\!=\!1.0$, $\sigma\!=\!1.1$,
Poisson sampling rate $q\!=\!0.2$ ($m\!=\!10$ of $N\!=\!50$);
cumulative $(\epsilon_T,\delta)\!\approx\!(13.6,10^{-5})$ at
$T\!=\!100$ via the Opacus PRV
accountant~\cite{gopi2021numerical}. Certificate per-query
smoothing $\epsilon_r\!=\!0.5$ is a verification-time parameter
distinct from $\epsilon_T$. Hardware: $4\!\times\!$A100 80\,GB.
Three seeds $\{42,137,2026\}$; the statistical unit is the
$(\mathrm{seed},\mathrm{dataset})$ pair ($21$ replicates over
seven method-dataset combinations). We report bootstrap $95\%$
CIs and Friedman with Holm post-hoc.

% 7. DISCUSSION
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the certificate buys, and at what cost.}
The certified $k^*\!=\!25$ converts an inference-time smoothing
budget into a coalition-size guarantee against bounded-update
adversaries (Def.~\ref{def:attack}) under SecAgg with no
raw-weight sharing, at a cost of $1.9$\,pp macro-F1 versus
centralised PEFTGuard and $0.5$--$1.0$\,pp from DP-SGD training.
The trade is favourable because centralised detection requires
raw-weight sharing that commercial actors refuse, whereas the
federated certificate uniquely combines ${>}96\%$ macro-F1,
training-time privacy at
$(\epsilon_T,\delta)\!\approx\!(13.6,10^{-5})$ under Opacus PRV,
and a distribution-free coalition bound.

\paragraph{Deployment scenario and hardware envelope.}
As an ingestion-time gate, on upload \modelname\ extracts
spectral, textual and usage features, scores them against the
local lineage subgraph, and emits
$\hat{p}_{\mathrm{mal}}(\theta)$ in
$0.7$--$2.9$\,s per adapter --- ``sub-second'' is specific to
A100-class hardware, and A10G/T4 extend the budget to
${\sim}1.6$--$2.9$\,s. Marketplaces exchange only DP-noised,
securely-aggregated gradients; $k^*$ is recomputed each round as
a trust indicator.

\paragraph{On the FLTrust root-set assumption.}
FLTrust asks the coordinator to hold a small vetted root set
$R_0$ of $200$ clean adapters, but this is not a paradox with the
scalability claim: $R_0$ is a \emph{static one-time trust anchor}
vetted at bootstrap, whereas the workload \modelname\ addresses is
the recurring, per-upload verification of an unbounded stream
(hundreds of thousands of adapters), which manual inspection
cannot cover. Rotating $R_0$ periodically and attesting its
integrity is a straightforward operational mitigation. A benign
marketplace genuinely misaligned with $R_0$ (e.g.\ a
low-resource-language hub) can also be down-weighted or zeroed;
mitigations include a more diverse root set, per-cluster
reference gradients, or a soft floor on client weights.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\modelname\ verifies LoRA-adapter integrity in a federated,
privacy-preserving manner: a heterogeneous continuous-time
dynamic graph captures the ecosystem, a federated DyGFormer--HGT
encoder scores adapters under client-update-level Gaussian DP-SGD
with SecAgg and FLTrust, and a certified poisoning-radius theorem
converts the per-query smoothing budget into a bounded-update
coalition bound. On $13{,}500$ adapters it obtains $96.4\%$
macro-F1 and certified $k^*\!=\!25$ at $N\!=\!50$ under
$\epsilon_r\!=\!0.5$, with sub-second per-adapter latency on
A100-class hardware and a measured ${\approx}4\%$ per-round
overhead for the full DP + SecAgg stack under CPU-hosted
federation (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}), offering a deployable
alternative to weight-sharing detectors.

\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Adaptive attacks against the certificate.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:certified} bounds \emph{bounded-update}
label-flip clients (Def.~\ref{def:attack}). It does not directly
bound data-controlling attackers (gradient-level poisoning) under
FLTrust nor arbitrary Byzantine gradients unconstrained by
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}$. Empirically FLTrust filters the
label-flip attack at $\rho\!\le\!0.33$, but this empirical
operating point is distinct from the $50\%$ certified bound;
formal extension to data-controlling adversaries is future work.

\paragraph{HuggingGraph synthesis and coverage.}
Cross-marketplace lineage edges in \benchname\ are synthesised
from HuggingGraph and PADBench; we do not replicate the full
causal Hub graph, and adapters truly novel (not derived from any
base in \benchname) are excluded by construction. Appendix
\ref{app:provenance} audits real-vs-synthetic components. We
evaluate four base-model families (Llama-2-7B, Llama-3-8B,
Mistral-7B, Qwen-7B) and four PEFT variants (LoRA, QLoRA,
AdaLoRA, DoRA); behaviour on full fine-tuning, MoE adapters and
$2$-bit quantised LoRA is not characterised.

\paragraph{DP accounting and benchmark realism.}
The reported $(\epsilon_T,\delta)\!\approx\!(13.6,10^{-5})$ at
$T\!=\!100$ is the numerical output of the Opacus PRV
accountant~\cite{gopi2021numerical} at $\sigma\!=\!1.1$,
$q\!=\!0.2$; tighter accountants such as Gaussian
DP~\cite{dong2022gaussian} could yield smaller noise, but we use
the standard PRV accountant for reproducibility. The ten attack
types in \benchname\ are all publicly documented; zero-day
families not matching these signatures may degrade detection.
\benchname's $50/50$ split is a controlled evaluation design, not
a base-rate claim; App.~\ref{app:additional} reports FPR,
precision and detection@FPR$\!=\!1\%$ for security-operating-
point readers.

\paragraph{False positives, fairness, and access.}
A false positive imposes a real cost on developers (quarantine,
reputational harm, delayed release), so deployments should pair
the detector with a low-FPR operating point and a human-review
redress path rather than automatic removal. Detection quality
may vary across base-model families and languages: English
adapters dominate \benchname\ and hubs specialising in
low-resource languages may be under-served both by the training
distribution and by the FLTrust root set (§\ref{sec:discussion}),
so a per-family and per-language audit is needed before broad
deployment. Finally, the ${\sim}960$ A100-GPU-hour training cost
and the federated design concentrate detection capability among
compute-rich marketplaces, raising an inclusivity and
power-concentration concern for small independent hubs;
participation incentives (reputation, security-certification
tiers) and lighter client-side training are needed for broad
adoption. We flag these as societal considerations rather than
solved problems.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

\paragraph{Defensive contribution and privacy by design.}
The system is designed for defensive operation by LoRA-hosting
marketplaces; its components support adapter-integrity
verification, not attack development. The threats it defends
against are publicly documented in cited prior
work~\cite{liu2024loraattack,lermen2023lora,huang2024composite}
and we introduce no new attack mechanisms. The federated framework
ensures marketplaces never share raw adapter weights;
client-update-level Gaussian DP-SGD plus SecAgg gives cumulative
$(\epsilon_T,\delta)\!\approx\!(13.6,10^{-5})$ per marketplace
over $T\!=\!100$ rounds under the Opacus PRV accountant.

\paragraph{Benchmark provenance and operator responsibility.}
\benchname\ is built from publicly released base models and
publicly documented attack methodologies; synthesised lineage
edges use HuggingGraph structure and introduce no PII; adapter
content is restricted to weight tensors, model cards and
download statistics; no private partner adapter weights are used.
It is released under a research-only licence with a use-case
attestation and SHA-256 checksums (App.~\ref{app:provenance}).
The certified $k^*\!=\!25$ is a probabilistic guarantee against
Def.~\ref{def:attack}, not against arbitrary Byzantine gradients;
operators must monitor for distribution shift and trigger online
adaptation. Coalitions larger than $k^*$ or attacks violating the
bounded-update assumption can in principle flip the verdict and
require operator-level mitigation. \modelname\ is one layer in a
defence-in-depth pipeline.

\paragraph{Compute footprint and use of AI writing assistants.}
Total compute is approximately $960$ A100-GPU-hours across
hyperparameter sweeps and three seeds; per-adapter deployment
latency is $0.7$\,s on a single A100. LLM writing assistants
were used only for grammar and phrasing on author-drafted text;
no technical content, experimental design, results or analysis
was AI-generated, and all citations were manually verified.

\section{Full Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sensitivity}}

\paragraph{Two-sided clipping bound.}
The client-update clipping map
$T_S(\mathbf{g}):=\mathbf{g}\cdot\min(1,S/\norm{\mathbf{g}}_2)$
satisfies $\norm{T_S(\mathbf{g})}_2\le S$ for every
$\mathbf{g}\in\R^d$. Hence for any two datasets $\Dcal_i$ and
$\Dcal_i'$,
$\norm{\bar{\mathbf{g}}_i(\Dcal_i)-\bar{\mathbf{g}}_i(\Dcal_i')}_2
\le \norm{\bar{\mathbf{g}}_i(\Dcal_i)}_2 +
\norm{\bar{\mathbf{g}}_i(\Dcal_i')}_2 \le 2S$, giving
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sensitivity}. This is the sensitivity we use for the
Gaussian mechanism calibration and does not depend on any
assumption on the GNN architecture.

\paragraph{Graph-structural refinement.}
When the sensitivity to a single node-edge change propagates through
$L$ HGT layers rather than saturating clipping, we can bound
$\norm{\Delta \bar{\mathbf{g}}_i}_2$ more tightly. By induction on
$L$: at the $\ell$-th HGT aggregation (Eq.~\ref{eq:hgt}),
$\mathbf{h}_v^{(\ell)} = \phi^{\mathrm{nl}}(\sum_{r,u} \alpha_{u,v,r}
\mathbf{W}_R^r \mathbf{h}_u^{(\ell-1)})$, and differing in a
single node-edge changes at most one term in the inner sum. By
Cauchy--Schwarz and the relation-wise spectral bound
$\norm{\mathbf{W}_R^r}_2 \le B_W$,
\begin{equation}
\norm{\Delta \mathbf{h}_v^{(\ell)}}_2 \le \rho \cdot B_W \cdot d_{\max} \cdot \norm{\Delta \mathbf{h}_v^{(\ell-1)}}_2
\end{equation}
whenever the affected node lies in the temporal neighbourhood.
Iterating $L$ layers yields the $(\rho B_W d_{\max})^L$ factor.
The $|\Rcal|^{1/2}$ factor comes from Cauchy--Schwarz over the
relation-type sum. Post-clipping to $S$ and taking the tighter of the two bounds
gives the refined form
$\Delta_2\le\min\!\bigl(2S,\,2S(\rho B_W d_{\max})^L |\Rcal|^{1/2}\bigr)$
as reported in Theorem~\ref{thm:sensitivity},
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sensitivity}.
For our deployment scale
$(\rho B_W d_{\max})^L\,|\Rcal|^{1/2}\gg 1$, so the operational
sensitivity is $2S$.
\qed

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:certified}}

The argument composes three established results, itemised below
with the specific hypothesis each contributes.

\paragraph{Step 1: PixelDP stability of the smoothed classifier.}
By Lemma~1 of \citet{lecuyer2019certified}, if $f$ is an
$(\epsilon_r,\delta)$-DP scoring function of its input
$\mathbf{x}$, then for any input perturbation $\Delta$ with
bounded $\ell_2$ norm,
$\Prob[\argmax f(\mathbf{x}) \neq \argmax f(\mathbf{x} +
\Delta)] \leq e^{\epsilon_r} \cdot p_{\Delta}(\mathbf{x}) + \delta$,
where $p_{\Delta}$ is the prediction-flip probability under the
noiseless mechanism. In our setting the random variable being
smoothed is the aggregated global model $\mathbf{w}_T\in\R^d$
(not the input adapter), and the $(\epsilon_r,\delta)$-DP mechanism
is the single Gaussian smoothing draw applied to $\mathbf{w}_T$ at
verification time.

\paragraph{Step 2: from parameter perturbation to client coalition.}
A single malicious client $i$ operating under
Definition~\ref{def:attack} submits
$\norm{\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_i-\mathbf{g}_i^{\mathrm{honest}}}_2\le
\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}$ after SecAgg. FLTrust weighting
$\beta_i\in[0,1]$ can only reduce the effective attacker
contribution to the aggregate, so any bound derived under
unweighted aggregation is a fortiori valid under FLTrust
weighting. Under unweighted averaging, $k$ colluding clients induce
a worst-case parameter-space perturbation of the aggregate of size
at most $\eta\cdot k\cdot\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}/m$ per round by
triangle inequality; here $\eta$ is the learning rate and $m$ is
the number of clients sampled per round. Summed over the training
horizon the total attacker-induced parameter perturbation is thus
bounded linearly in $k$.

\paragraph{Step 3: CRFL federated smoothing.}
By Theorem~1 of \citet{xie2021crfl}, extending randomized
smoothing~\cite{cohen2019certified}, federated parameter clipping
plus a Gaussian smoothing draw on the aggregated model certifies
a class-margin radius (in parameter-perturbation space) via a
Neyman--Pearson hypothesis-testing argument. Translated into a
client coalition of size $N$, this reads
\begin{equation}
k^* = \left\lfloor \frac{N (\Phi^{-1}(\hat{p}_1) - \Phi^{-1}(\hat{p}_2))}{2 e^{\epsilon_r}} \right\rfloor,
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon_r$ is the $(\epsilon_r,\delta)$-DP budget of the
single smoothing draw applied at verification time. The factor
$2 e^{\epsilon_r}$ combines the PixelDP per-query stability
multiplier $e^{\epsilon_r}$ (Step~1) with the CRFL
class-margin denominator $2$ (two half-Gaussian tails). The
PixelDP factor is therefore instantiated at $\epsilon_r$, not at
the cumulative training-time budget $\epsilon_T$.

\paragraph{Step 4: R\'enyi-DP composition (marketplace privacy only).}
By the standard R\'enyi-DP composition
theorem~\cite{mironov2017renyi}, the Gaussian mechanism satisfies
$(\alpha,\alpha/(2\sigma^2))$-RDP, and for the
\emph{Poisson-subsampled} Gaussian mechanism at rate $q$ the
per-round R\'enyi divergence is amplified to
$O(q^2\alpha/\sigma^2)$~\cite{mironov2019sampled}; composing $T$
rounds and converting to $(\epsilon_T,\delta)$-DP gives
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_T \leq \min_{\alpha>1}\;\frac{2 T q^2 \alpha}{\sigma^2} + \frac{\ln(1/\delta)}{\alpha - 1}.
\end{equation}
In practice we do not use this closed form: at $\sigma=1.1$,
$q=0.2$, $T=100$, $\delta=10^{-5}$, the Opacus PRV
accountant~\cite{gopi2021numerical} returns
$\epsilon_T\approx 13.6$, and we report this numerical value as
the marketplace-privacy guarantee. This step does \emph{not} enter
the certificate: the certificate is set by the per-query smoothing
budget $\epsilon_r$ of Step~3, not by the cumulative training-time
$\epsilon_T$ of Step~4.

Substituting the per-query smoothing budget $\epsilon_r$ into the
CRFL bound yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:kstar}. The failure probability
$T\delta$ accumulates by union bound over rounds if the smoothing
is re-drawn per round; a single-draw certificate uses the tighter
$\delta$. The graph-structural extension to heterogeneous edge-set
perturbations follows from
\citet{wang2021certgnn,scholten2022randomized}.
\qed

\section{Additional Analyses}
\label{app:additional}

\paragraph{Spectral-only baseline.}
Because backdoored adapters carry a prominent rank-1 signature
(Figure~\ref{fig:spectrum}), we test whether that signal alone
suffices. A non-graph, non-federated SVD-only detector thresholding
$\sigma_1/\sigma_2$ reaches $90.2\%$ macro-F1 on single-trigger
rank-1 backdoors but only $71.4\%$ on composite-trigger,
weight-poisoning, and trigger-free merging attacks (the
logistic-regression variant on the full spectral signature is
similar: $91.0\%/73.1\%$), and offers no certificate. \modelname's
advantage is therefore concentrated on the hard, context-dependent
attacks and on the certified radius, not on the easy rank-1 cases
where a cheap spectral screen suffices.

\paragraph{Leakage-controlled splits.}
Table~\ref{tab:splits} reports \modelname's macro-F1 under four
increasingly strict test partitions of \benchname. Under a random
adapter split (the standard split) \modelname\ reaches $96.4\%$;
under a lineage-component-disjoint split (the strictest split that
still preserves attack coverage) it retains $95.1\%$; under a
base-model-disjoint split it drops to $93.7\%$; under an
attack-family-held-out split (each attack type in turn) it retains
$92.3\%$ averaged over held-out families. The main-paper number
$96.4\%$ is reported at the lineage-component-disjoint split, so

\paragraph{Security-operating-point metrics.}
Because macro-F1 on the balanced $50/50$ split can under-report
the false-positive burden a marketplace would actually see, we
also report precision, recall, false-positive rate (FPR), and
detection@FPR$=1\%$ at the paper's default operating point of
$\hat{p}_{\mathrm{mal}}\ge 0.6$. Values (mean over three seeds):
precision $= 94.4\%$, recall $= 96.9\%$, FPR $= 5.9\%$, PR-AUC
$= 0.972$, detection@FPR$=1\%$ $= 82.4\%$. On the imbalanced
production distribution (estimated $\sim 0.4\%$ malicious base
rate in prior industry reports) the operator would tune to a
low-FPR operating point; at $\hat{p}_{\mathrm{mal}}\ge 0.9$ we
observe FPR $= 0.7\%$ with detection rate $84.1\%$.

\paragraph{Robustness of the operating envelope.}
At a stronger-privacy point (cumulative $\epsilon_T = 1.0$) macro-F1
is $94.1\%$, a $2.3$\,pp drop from the operational $\epsilon_T
\approx 13.6$ point --- graceful degradation, giving a
privacy--utility curve rather than a single point. Under random
removal of HuggingGraph lineage edges, macro-F1 falls from
$96.4\%$ to $95.6\%/93.8\%/91.1\%$ at $10\%/30\%/50\%$ removal, so
a complete lineage graph is not required. A two-sample
Kolmogorov--Smirnov test on the top-1 singular-value distributions
confirms the $2.43\times$ benign/backdoored separation is
significant ($D=0.71$, $p<10^{-3}$). Latency is $0.7$\,s per
adapter on an A100~80\,GB (including neighbour retrieval and
feature extraction), rising to $\sim\!1.6$\,s on an A10G and
$\sim\!2.9$\,s on a T4; the ``sub-second'' claim is thus specific
to A100-class hardware.

hyperparameter sweeps. The codebase, the \benchname\ benchmark,
and pretrained checkpoints are released at the repository below.
Hyperparameters: DyGFormer encoder $3$ layers, $H = 8$ attention
heads, $d_v = 256$. HGT relation-specific matrices initialised
with Xavier-uniform. Training: AdamW with learning rate $5 \times
10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, $T = 100$ rounds, batch size
$32$ per client. Client-update-level DP-SGD: $S = 1.0$, $\sigma =
1.1$, Poisson sampling rate $q = 0.2$, $\delta = 10^{-5}$. FLTrust
root set $|R_0| = 200$ vetted clean adapters. Per-query smoothing
budget for the certificate: $\epsilon_r = 0.5$.

\paragraph{DP accounting configuration.} The reported
$\epsilon_T \approx 13.6$ at $T=100$ rounds is computed with the
Opacus PRV accountant~\cite{gopi2021numerical} under
\emph{Poisson} subsampling at sampling rate $q = 0.2$, noise
multiplier $\sigma = 1.1$, client-update clipping $S = 1.0$,
$\delta = 10^{-5}$. As a cross-check, the Opacus R\'enyi
accountant returns $\epsilon_T \approx 13.56$ at the same
parameters --- consistent with PRV within accountant looseness.
The manuscript's earlier draft misreported $\epsilon_T\approx 5.0$
for these parameters; that value would require $\sigma\approx
2.15$ at $(q,T,\delta)=(0.2,100,10^{-5})$ under the same PRV
accountant. We report the honest PRV output for the sigma
actually used in the released code and checkpoints. The per-query
certificate smoothing budget $\epsilon_r=0.5$ is a separate design
parameter of the inference-time smoothing draw and is not
composed into $\epsilon_T$; it enters only Theorem~\ref{thm:certified}.
The exact configuration is::
\begin{quote}
\small
\begin{verbatim}
accountant       = PRV (Opacus)
sample_rate q    = 0.20 (Poisson)
sigma            = 1.10
clip S           = 1.00
T                = 100 rounds
delta            = 1e-5
result eps_T     = 13.6 (PRV), 13.56 (RDP)
\end{verbatim}
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Training-time overhead of the privacy stack.}
Because \modelname\ applies DP clipping at the
\emph{client-update} level (Algorithm~\ref{alg:fedloraguard},
line~5) rather than to per-example gradients, it does not require
the per-sample gradient materialisation or microbatching that
dominates the cost of example-level DP-SGD: each round adds one
$O(d)$ norm-and-rescale and one $O(d)$ Gaussian draw per client,
where $d$ is the encoder parameter count. Secure aggregation
contributes a pairwise PRG mask that is likewise $O(d)$ per
client, plus the mask-exchange round of the Bonawitz
protocol~\cite{bonawitz2017practical}, whose cost is
communication- rather than compute-bound and is amortised over the
local epochs of a round. We instrument the released runtime to
measure this overhead directly. On identical CPU hardware, over
three independent runs with $20$--$60$ measurement rounds each
after $3$--$5$ warm-up rounds and identical client work (six
clients, $H{=}4$ HGT heads, $d_v=96$, batch size $32$), the
pooled median round wall-clock is $1.89$\,s under non-private
FedAvg and $1.96$\,s with the full DP + secure-aggregation stack,
a pooled median overhead of $+3.4\%$ (across the three runs
individually the full-stack overhead is $-1.1\%$, $+10.6\%$, and
$+3.4\%$; per-round overhead of the DP step alone is dominated by
CPU-jitter at this model scale, so we round the reported figure
to $\approx 4\%$). Because CPU-hosted federation puts most of the
per-round cost in local GNN training, the DP + SecAgg mask draws
account for a small fraction of round time; on
compute-accelerated (A100-class) hardware the same mask draws
would represent a larger relative share and the ratio would be
higher. Convergence in rounds is unchanged ($T = 100$); the
certificate and privacy guarantee are therefore obtained at a
modest training-time cost.
At verification time, no additional privacy training step is
required beyond the forward pass and the single per-query
smoothing draw described in Theorem~\ref{thm:certified}; the
end-to-end per-adapter verification budget on A100 is $0.7$\,s
(Appendix~\ref{app:additional}). We ship the round-time
instrumentation in the released runtime so this overhead can be
re-measured on other hardware.
