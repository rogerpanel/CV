# Borrow proven modules, then measure SODE-Guard honestly

SODE-Guard can plausibly reach parity on all three targets. Three borrowed, well-evidenced modules carry most of that, and the stochastic dynamics carry almost none of it. First, imbalance-aware training with a realistic budget fixes clean macro-F1: logit-adjusted or deferred-reweighted loss, class-balanced sampling, quantile or piecewise-linear feature encoding, and far more than 10 epochs with early stopping on validation macro-F1. Second, adversarial training through the full 8-path Euler-Maruyama solve, with an EOT-matched adversary, closes the worst-case gap to the PGD-AT MLP. Third, certifying with randomized smoothing instead of Theorem A closes most of the certified gap. A pure-diffusion prefix on the input makes the SDE's own noise a valid smoothing distribution, and that construction is sound but unpublished. The evidence behind this ordering is consistent across domains. Every documented "weak model later matched the benchmark" story the researchers found turned on training hygiene, input representation, an established robustness mechanism (adversarial training or smoothing), or a corrected evaluation. None turned on the novel inductive bias alone. The group's own two examples fit the same pattern. MambaGuard did not beat its bag-of-words baseline, and BlueSec's safeguards had no measurable effect. Each paper's contribution was rigorous negative measurement plus one or two modules that clearly helped. Matching the adversarially trained MLP on worst-case macro-F1 and matching smoothing on certified accuracy are realistic targets. Beating either by more than seed noise is not supported by any evidence found. A deterministic Theorem A certificate will not reach smoothing's 0.606 at radius 0.273. And SODE-Guard with its learned diffusion should not be expected to beat a Neural ODE given the same modules. One caveat applies throughout. The research sessions could not open arXiv, OpenReview, PMLR, USENIX, NDSS or Kaggle full texts, so every literature number below comes from search snippets and must be checked against the PDFs before it goes into a paper.

## Clean macro-F1 caps both robustness metrics, so the first gap is not adversarial

The rebuttal numbers describe three separate problems, and they have to be fixed in order. All six models reach 90-94% accuracy but only 0.41-0.46 clean macro-F1. That pattern means the majority classes are right and most of the six small classes are close to F1 zero. That ceiling binds everything downstream. The best worst-case model, the PGD-AT MLP, loses only 0.026 macro-F1 under attack (0.409 clean to 0.383 worst-case), so its robust score is capped by its clean score, not by the attack. The l-inf budget of 0.03 in z-score space is about 3% of a standard deviation per feature. The researchers' reading is that SODE-Guard's collapse from 0.429 to 0.225 reflects missing local smoothness rather than an intrinsically hard threat model, because one round of adversarial training nearly removes the gap for an MLP. Comparable CICFlowMeter-style datasets give tree ensembles a macro-F1 floor around 0.6-0.9, depending on dataset and split. Examples include UNSW-NB15 XGBoost at about 0.67 under 5-fold CV ([IIETA](https://www.iieta.org/download/file/fid/197013)), CIC-IDS2017 RF at 0.769 under a temporal split ([arXiv 2606.11098](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11098v2)) and Edge-IIoTset RF at 0.925 ([arXiv 2510.26941](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26941)). On that evidence, **0.43 is a training failure, not a property of neural SDEs**.

The worst-case numbers point the same way. Without adversarial training, SODE-Guard (0.225), the Neural ODE with g=0 (0.227) and the plain MLP (0.238) are statistically indistinguishable at a glance. The learned diffusion "barely changes predictions". The anti-concentration regulariser did nothing. This matches the literature closely. Parametric Noise Injection, which learns its noise scale under min-max adversarial training, still falls to EOT gradient estimation, and EOT "is usually more effective when dealing with noise inside the network" ([arXiv 2206.01705](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.01705v1.pdf)). Learned noise scales also tend to shrink toward zero unless adversarial pressure or a noise-promoting regulariser holds them up, which is what SODE-Guard's g did. The notes found no paper in which a neural-SDE diffusion term adds robustness that survives EOT or AutoAttack.

The certificate gap is structural. Theorem A multiplies the readout and encoder norms by exp((K_f + 0.5 K_g² + …)T). That product gives L=11.5 for SODE-Guard against 5.1 for the Neural ODE and 1.4 for the Lipschitz MLP. Certified accuracy at r=0.273 tracks L exactly: 0.096, 0.280 and 0.422. Randomized smoothing at σ=0.25 needs no network Lipschitz bound and holds 0.606 there. Certified accuracy at radius 0 (0.61-0.64) is lower than the 90-94% clean accuracy for a known reason: the certificates were computed on 500 class-stratified flows, so the six poorly learned small classes carry far more weight than in the full test set. Abstentions also count as errors. The radius-0 figure is therefore close to a class-balanced accuracy, not the overall accuracy. The protocol below fixes one stratified subset for all certificates and also reports the unweighted figure.

## Weak models caught up through training, inputs and evaluation, never through the novel bias

### The group's own examples turned negative results into the contribution

Neither of the two attached manuscripts "beat benchmarks" through new modules, and that is the most important lesson they offer. In **MambaGuard** (agent-protocol traffic, S&P 2027 submission), a bag-of-words baseline matched or beat the structured state-space detector on all five benchmarks. The one module that clearly helped was **chunk-level multiple-instance payload scoring with max+mean pooling, worth +0.20 to +0.38 macro-F1**. It helped because it changed what evidence the detector aggregates, not the backbone. The rest of the stack was standard: focal loss, class-balanced sampling, a spectral-norm penalty, AdamW, early stopping on a selection-half validation split, and Platt calibration, which fixed ECE. Several components produced honest partial failures. The calibrated detector + lexical + Mahalanobis-novelty hybrid beat lexical scoring in only 11 of 71 held-out folds. Split-conformal alarm thresholds overshot the false-alarm target threefold on new servers, and adaptive conformal inference restored false-alarm control at a cost in recall. A naive block-product Lipschitz bound turned out to be unsound and was replaced by a residual-aware one, and the smoothing certificate was audited for its radius ceiling. The statistics were what made the paper trustworthy: five seeds, paired bootstrap, Holm correction, and grouped and held-out-family splits. **An ablation winner chosen on two seeds lost 0.34 macro-F1 when re-run on five.**

**BlueSec** (LLM alert triage on provenance graphs, S&P 2027 submission) has the same shape. Its call-level safeguards had no measurable effect, and the paper reported that null result with statistics. Its prompted investigation method cut tool calls by about 30%. Cue-controlled "blind" and "mimicry" variants exposed a name-based shortcut: renaming entities raised missed attacks from 2/45 to 9/45. Both papers earned their credibility from honest ablations and shortcut controls, with one or two modules that genuinely moved a metric. SODE-Guard should be planned the same way. The paper needs one module whose gain survives five seeds and Holm correction, sitting inside an evaluation that a hostile reviewer cannot break.

### Published turnarounds follow four routes, none of them architectural novelty

The external record shows the same thing from four directions.

The first route is training and regularisation. Plain MLPs were long considered weak on tabular data. Kadra et al. searched a per-dataset "regularisation cocktail" over 13 regularisers on 40 datasets, and the resulting MLPs **statistically significantly outperformed XGBoost and CatBoost** ([NeurIPS 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/c902b497eb972281fb5b4e206db38ee6-Abstract.html)).

The second route is input representation. Piecewise-linear and periodic numerical embeddings let simple MLPs match attention models and compete with GBDTs on some GBDT-friendly benchmarks ([Gorishniy et al., NeurIPS 2022](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/9e9f0ffc3d836836ca96cbf8fe14b105-Abstract.html)). Whisper kept Kitsune's autoencoder, replaced packet-level features with frequency-domain ones, and **gained up to 15.72% AUC** ([arXiv 2106.14707](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.14707)). This is the closest network-traffic analogue of MambaGuard's chunk scoring. TabM, now reported as the strongest tabular DL model, is an efficient ensemble of MLPs. Its authors conclude that "the complexity of attention- and retrieval-based methods does not pay off" ([TabM, ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/c1ba41c694834aeef91ae161711d4939-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

The third route is the established robustness mechanism. When Athalye, Carlini and Wagner broke seven of the ICLR 2018 defences, **PGD adversarial training was the only one that held, at 47% CIFAR-10 accuracy at l-inf ε=0.031** ([arXiv 1802.00420](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.00420v2)). That ε is almost exactly SODE-Guard's budget. Continuous-depth models repeat the story. Stabilised neural ODEs looked robust only because the adaptive DOPRI5 solver masked gradients: PGD failed, while CW and gradient-free SPSA succeeded ([Huang et al., PMLR v145](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v145/huang22a.html)). SODEF's self-reported AutoAttack gain over TRADES (53.69% to 57.76%, [GitHub](https://github.com/KANGQIYU/SODEF)) reversed under surrogate-loss transfer, which left SODEF **less robust than its own TRADES base** ([Croce et al., ICML 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.13711)).

The fourth route is evaluation correction. Arp et al. showed that a boxplot outlier detector on packet frequency matched Kitsune and beat it at low false-positive rates ([arXiv 2010.09470](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.09470)). Engelen et al. reconstructed or relabelled more than 20% of CICIDS2017 traces, and benchmark results changed significantly ([researchportal.be](https://researchportal.be/en/node/8038227)). TESSERACT showed near-0.99 malware F1 inflated by spatial and temporal bias ([USENIX 2019](https://usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity19/presentation/pendlebury)). TRANSCENDENT failed to reproduce Transcend's claims at first, then fixed them with new conformal evaluators ([arXiv 2010.03856](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.03856)).

Two lessons follow for SODE-Guard. Any gain it reports must come from a nameable module that the Neural ODE and MLP baselines can also receive. If they receive it and match SODE-Guard, the honest claim becomes the framework plus the evaluation, as in MambaGuard.

## Imbalance correction and tabular training hygiene are the first, cheapest win

With a 380:1 imbalance (about 50,000 vs 131 flows), 10 epochs of plain cross-entropy give the smallest class only a handful of gradient contributions. Long-tail theory and the scattered NIDS evidence agree on what to change. The main options:

- **Logit adjustment.** In post-hoc form it subtracts τ·log prior at inference. In loss form it is equivalent to Balanced Softmax at τ=1. Post-hoc adjustment cut balanced error by 8% (relative) against weight normalisation on CIFAR-10 ([Menon et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.07314)).
- **LDAM-DRW.** Larger margins for rare classes, with reweighting deferred until the learning rate anneals. Its authors found deferred reweighting more effective than reweighting from the start ([Cao et al., NeurIPS 2019](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/621461af90cadfdaf0e8d4cc25129f91-Abstract.html)).
- **Class-balanced weights by effective number** ([Cui et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.05555)). As β approaches 1 these become inverse-frequency weights, so at 380:1 they need clipping or deferral.

On NIDS data, loss-level fixes were reported to lift CIC-IDS2017 macro-F1 from 0.618 to 0.756 after a baseline CNN scored F1=0 on Heartbleed ([IJEECS](https://ijeecs.iaescore.com/index.php/IJEECS/article/view/45292)). That paper attribution is approximate and needs checking. CTGAN oversampling lifted NF-CSE-CIC-IDS2018 macro-F1 from 0.678 to 0.780, against 0.697 for SMOTE ([arXiv 2502.06031](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.06031)). CTGAN, however, also destroyed adversarial robustness for several architectures on CTU-13 in TabularBench ([TabularBench](https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/63141/1/tabular_bench_benchmarking_adversarial_robustness_for_tabular_deep_learning_in_real_world_use_cases.pdf)). That makes generative oversampling a poor fit for a paper whose second metric is robustness. Post-hoc logit or per-class bias tuning costs nothing and can be applied to the existing rebuttal checkpoints today. The tuning must use a validation split, never test.

The tabular-DL literature adds a second layer. Heavy-tailed flow counts and durations survive z-scoring as extreme outliers. A quantile transform, log1p before scaling, or piecewise-linear encoding (which is quantile-binned) handles them by construction. Grinsztajn et al. attribute trees' persistent advantage on medium-sized data to NNs' weakness with uninformative features, rotation-invariant processing and irregular targets ([arXiv 2207.08815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08815)), so pruning constant or near-duplicate columns among the 75 should help the SDE more than it helps a GBDT. One SDE-specific opportunity stands out. The 8-path mean-logit predictor already pays for eight forward passes, but with a collapsed diffusion the paths are near-identical, so most of that compute buys nothing. Replacing them with TabM-style BatchEnsemble members gives genuine diversity at roughly the same cost: shared drift weights plus per-member rank-1 adapters, with each member running its own Euler-Maruyama solve. TabM is the documented mechanism behind the strongest current tabular MLPs ([arXiv 2410.24210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.24210)). Applying it inside an SDE is an inference and has not been tested.

The ceiling here is uncertain. The researchers found no published result on ICS3D at all, so the GBDT score on these exact splits is unknown. If a tuned GBDT scores above about 0.9, the first step is to check for duplicates and identifier leakage, not to claim parity.

## Adversarial training is the robustness mechanism, and diffusion should be demoted

The in-house evidence is already decisive. Adversarial training took the MLP's worst-case macro-F1 from 0.238 to 0.383 at almost no clean cost. Nothing comparable has been tried on SODE-Guard with plain cross-entropy. The first robustness module is therefore **PGD adversarial training through the full stochastic forward pass**. The adversary must match the evaluator. If training attacks see one noisy path while evaluation uses EOT over 8 or 256 paths, the model learns to rely on gradient noise that the evaluator averages away. Concretely, the attack should step on the mean logit over the same M paths with a fixed-step solver and full backprop, not the adjoint. Fixed-step Euler-Maruyama is an advantage: it avoids the adaptive-solver masking that inflated SONet and Carrara's results ([Carrara et al., Information 2022](https://mdpi.com/2078-2489/13/12/555)), so whatever robustness training produces will be real.

Cost is the binding constraint, because every attack step is a full forward and backward solve. N-FGSM avoids catastrophic overfitting and matches GradAlign robustness at a 3x speed-up ([NeurIPS 2022](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/5434a6b40f8f65488e722bc33d796c8b-Paper-Conference.pdf)), at about 2 solve-pairs per step against 6 to 11 for PGD-5 to PGD-10. One evaluation reports N-FGSM overfitting catastrophically at large ε ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=lifRwnIuAv0)). ε=0.03 in z-space is small, so the risk is modest, but N-FGSM should be checked against a short PGD-10 run. GradAlign needs double backprop through the solver and is the worst fit. Add-ons beyond the base AT recipe are worth only a few points. On images, AWP added about 2.5-3 AutoAttack points, reaching 56.17% against 53.51% for a comparable baseline ([AutoAttack leaderboard](https://github.com/fra31/auto-attack)), and TRADES about 1 point. Rice et al. found that **early stopping alone matched "virtually all recent algorithmic improvements"** over adversarial training ([ICML 2020](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/rice20a/rice20a.pdf)). With 70k rows and a 131-flow class, robust overfitting will be fast. Early stopping on PGD-20+EOT macro-F1 from the selection-half validation split is mandatory.

Imbalance has to be built into the adversarial loss, because adversarial training amplifies class disparity. On CIFAR-10, PGD-AT leaves "cat" at 82.4% robust error against a 56.4% average ([Xu et al., ICML 2021](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/xu21b/xu21b.pdf)). The cheapest fixes are a class-balanced or logit-adjusted outer loss and balanced generation of adversarial examples, following REAT's rebalancing loss ([arXiv 2307.10205](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.10205)). Per-class ε and TRADES β (CFA, [CVPR 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Wei_CFA_Class-Wise_Calibrated_Fair_Adversarial_Training_CVPR_2023_paper.html)) are a second step. TabularBench warns that some apparently robust tabular models just predict one class ([TabularBench](https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/63141/1/tabular_bench_benchmarking_adversarial_robustness_for_tabular_deep_learning_in_real_world_use_cases.pdf)). Macro-F1 already guards against that, but per-class worst-case recall should be reported as well.

The diffusion term should be demoted from defence to ablation variable. If it is kept, fix a floor (g = σ_min + softplus(·)) or a constant additive σ so that it cannot collapse. Then compare g=0, fixed σ and learned g under identical adversarial training. Stability modules such as SODEF equilibria, Lyapunov losses and TisODE steady-state penalties have at most a few points of evidence, all of it contested. They belong in an appendix ablation, not the main method.

## Certify through smoothing; repair Theorem A as a secondary track

The largest certified-accuracy lever is to stop relying on a global Lipschitz product. Cohen et al.'s Neyman-Pearson certificate, R = σ/2·(Φ⁻¹(p_A) − Φ⁻¹(p_B)) with a Clopper-Pearson lower bound on p_A ([arXiv 1902.02918](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.02918)), holds for any base classifier, randomized ones included, as long as the extra randomness is independent of the input. That yields a sound construction the notes derived but did not find published. On t ∈ [0, τ], set f=0 and g=σ₀I on the input, so that z(τ) = x + σ₀√τ·ε is exactly Gaussian smoothing with σ_RS = σ₀√τ. Everything after τ, including learned drift and further noise, becomes part of the randomized base classifier.

Three conditions follow. The certifying noise must be additive and isotropic, because state-dependent g at the first injection breaks the Gaussian shift. If noise is injected after the encoder instead, the input radius divides by L_h, in the PixelDP pattern ([arXiv 1802.03471](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.03471)). And the certified predictor is the smoothed classifier, either a vote or the mean softmax, which Salman et al. show is also certifiable ([arXiv 1906.04584](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04584)). The current mean-logit is not directly certifiable. The novelty claim must be modest, since this is randomized smoothing implemented by an SDE. In return it plausibly lets SODE-Guard reach the 0.606 that MLP smoothing achieves at r=0.273, provided the base model stays accurate under σ_RS noise.

Training the base model for smoothing helps less than switching certificates. On CIFAR-10 at σ=0.25, the ACR gap between MACER, Consistency, SmoothMix and CAT-RS is 0.531 to 0.562 ([CAT-RS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.09000)), and the rankings flip across protocols ([arXiv 2310.07780](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.07780)). Gaussian augmentation at σ_RS is the minimum. Consistency regularisation is cheap here because the paths are already sampled. A 2026 traffic-specific preprint reports that clean-trained models certify poorly and noise-augmented fine-tuning is essential ([arXiv 2607.13801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13801v1)). Feature-wise noise in the style of BARS and MARS ([BARS, NDSS 2023](https://ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/bars-local-robustness-certification-for-deep-learning-based-traffic-analysis-systems); [MARS](https://mengdiehuang.github.io/slides/TrustCom2024-MARS-15min-Presentation-Slides-20241218.pdf)) certifies an ellipsoid, not an l2 ball. It is acceptable only if the threat model says so explicitly.

The deterministic track is worth pursuing for the paper's theory, but it cannot win at r=0.273: even the L=1.4 Lipschitz MLP reaches only 0.422. Three changes cut L.

- **Additive diffusion.** With additive diffusion and a shared Brownian path, two trajectories obey d(z₁−z₂)/dt = f(z₁) − f(z₂). Their separation is then bounded by exp(μT), where μ bounds the log-norm of ∂f/∂z, and the 0.5 K_g² term disappears.
- **A non-expansive drift.** For example, f(z) = −αz + W₂σ(W₁z) with α ≥ ‖W₂‖‖W₁‖L_σ gives μ ≤ 0 and reduces L to ‖W_ψ‖L_h. Orthogonal, AOL or SLL layers for W_ψ and h can push that to 1 or below. These first two points are standard contraction theory as applied by the researchers. They are not a published SODE-Guard result and need a careful proof check.
- **Training on the certificate's L.** Train with an LMT or GloRo margin loss using the same L as Theorem A, so training optimises the certified margin directly.

The closest published certified neural ODE, FI-ODE, reaches 42.27% at l2 ε=0.141 on CIFAR-10 ([arXiv 2210.16940](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.16940)). Contraction does carry an expressivity cost.

The existing Monte-Carlo certificate also needs an audit. It is sound only if every path's logit map shares the Lipschitz constant, the margin uses confidence bounds with a union bound over 9 classes, and the logits are bounded for Hoeffding (or variance-controlled for Bernstein). Reviewers will check all three.

## A prioritised module plan, costed in solver passes

Costs use **C = one clean training step: forward plus backward through the 8-path Euler-Maruyama solve at S steps**. Cost scales linearly in S and in the path count M. An adversarial step on the 8-path mean logit costs about one extra C. "Evidence strength" grades the support for the expected effect *on this kind of problem*. "Strong" means replicated, or in-house evidence on ICS3D. "Moderate" means solid but from another domain. "Weak" means snippets, a single study, or inference.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 1 | Post-hoc logit adjustment / per-class bias | Tune τ or per-class biases on the validation half; apply to existing checkpoints | Immediate rare-class recall gain, no retraining (moderate: Menon; untested on NIDS) | 0 | τ=0 vs tuned τ, all models |
| 2 | Training budget | AdamW, cosine LR, 50-100 epochs, early stopping on val macro-F1, EMA | Large clean gain over 10 epochs (moderate: Kadra; 10 epochs is far below norm) | C (more steps) | 10 vs full budget, same seed set |
| 3 | Imbalance loss + sampling | Logit-adjusted CE or LDAM-DRW; class-balanced sampler with clipped weights; no CTGAN | Largest clean macro-F1 module; NIDS reports +0.07 to +0.14 (moderate-weak: snippet attributions) | ≈C | CE vs LA vs LDAM-DRW vs focal, ×{sampler on/off} |

Modules 1-4 and 6 should come first, in that order. Without 1-4, adversarial training will lock in a model whose clean ceiling is 0.43. Module 9 is the only route to the smoothing target. Module 11 is the only candidate for a gain large enough to be the paper's positive headline. It depends on ICS3D retaining container, pod or time identifiers, which the 75 z-scored features may not include, and that needs checking. CROWN-IBP bound propagation on the discretised drift is feasible at 75 dimensions, and GAINS is the only established certified-training method for NODEs ([arXiv 2303.05246](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.05246)). It is optional and expensive, and it gives at best a tighter local bound for track #10.

## A re-run protocol that a hostile reviewer cannot break

**Data provenance comes first.** ICS3D appears to be a Kaggle compilation by the account rogernickanaedevha that bundles a container misuse dataset with Edge-IIoTset ([Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/rogernickanaedevha/integrated-cloud-security-3datasets-ics3d)). The page was seen only through a search summary, and no peer-reviewed paper or reported result on ICS3D was found. The container part plausibly derives from the METU Kubernetes misuse dataset of Sever and Doğan (10 CVE-based attack scenarios plus benign; [ITU J-FET 2023](https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-s/opb/jnl/S-JNL-VOL4.ISSUE2-2023-A26-PDF-E.pdf); [Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/yigitsever/misuse-detection-in-containers-dataset)). That source would give 11 classes against ICS3D's 9, so merged or dropped scenarios must be identified. Who compiled ICS3D is not established, and the Kaggle account may belong to the authors. The paper should cite Sever and Doğan, state the class mapping, and treat the original METU release as the second dataset, which also enables a cross-dataset transfer test.

| Component | Specification |
|---|---|
| Splits | Exact-duplicate removal, then a stratified train / validation / test split (60/20/20). Group by container or episode if identifiers exist. Split validation into a selection half (early stopping, τ, β) and a calibration half (temperature, conformal). The global temporal split is degenerate, so replace it with a per-class chronological split (early part of each class's time span to train, late part to test), labelled as a robustness check. Leave-one-attack-family-out for novelty. Train on ICS3D, test on METU original, after schema harmonisation. |

## Two parity targets are plausible; three common hopes are not

The plausible outcomes are concrete. **Clean macro-F1 well above 0.43** should follow from modules 1-4 alone. Parity with TabM or RealMLP is a reasonable goal. Parity with a tuned GBDT is uncertain, given the tree advantage documented on medium-sized tabular data. **Worst-case macro-F1 equal to the PGD-AT MLP within confidence intervals** is plausible once SODE-Guard is adversarially trained with an EOT-matched adversary. The AT-MLP's small clean-to-robust gap implies that at this ε, worst-case largely follows clean macro-F1, so raising both models' clean scores raises the bar together. **Certified accuracy matching randomized smoothing** is plausible by certifying through a pure-diffusion prefix. This is the same certificate, so parity is the ceiling. Training-method differences between smoothing variants sit at about 0.01-0.03 ACR, which leaves little room to beat it.

The implausible outcomes deserve equal clarity. No source shows adversarial training helping ODE or SDE models more than MLPs under fully adaptive evaluation, so **beating AT-MLP on worst-case by more than seed noise** has no evidential basis. **A deterministic Theorem A certificate matching smoothing at r=0.273** is ruled out by the in-house numbers: a 1-Lipschitz-class MLP reaches 0.422 there. Contraction can make Theorem A competitive with that MLP, and that is a respectable theoretical result in its own right. **Learned diffusion adding robustness** contradicts both the in-house null and the PNI/EOT literature. Finally, if every gain comes from modules the Neural ODE also receives, SODE-Guard will not beat the Neural ODE. The paper should then be written the way MambaGuard was. It would present a rigorously evaluated framework showing that stochastic dynamics add nothing under adaptive attack, with the borrowed modules quantified. The open niche supports that framing: the researchers found no neural ODE or SDE intrusion-detection work with any adaptive robustness evaluation.

Several numbers came from search snippets, not PDFs, and must be verified before they are quoted. They include the TRADES, AWP and CFA gains, AWP's 8% overhead, the TabularBench CTU-13 rows, the NIDS imbalance-loss gains and their paper attributions, the TabM rankings, the SLL erratum figures, FI-ODE's 42.27%, the BARS and MARS results, and the "Do Transformers Help IDS" split effect. Hosts that need allowing to fetch the full texts are arxiv.org, openreview.net, proceedings.mlr.press, usenix.org, ndss-symposium.org and kaggle.com. The Kaggle page in particular is needed to settle ICS3D's class list and licence.

## Conclusion

The rebuttal's failure is better news than it looks. Its root cause is under-training on a 380:1 imbalanced problem, plus a certificate that multiplies worst-case constants. Neither problem is intrinsic to neural SDEs, and both have off-the-shelf remedies whose costs are known in solver passes. What the evidence does not support is the original premise that stochastic dynamics confer robustness. Every comparable claim in images collapsed under EOT, transfer or gradient-free attacks, and SODE-Guard's own diffusion has already collapsed under clean training.

The strategic choice is therefore about framing more than modules. One option is to recast the SDE as an implementation of smoothing and ensembling, an honest but modest contribution. The other is to look for the place where continuous-time dynamics could earn their keep: per-container flow sequences scored as bags, the one module family whose analogue moved macro-F1 by tenths in the group's own work. In both cases the paper's credibility will come from the same features MambaGuard and BlueSec relied on: five seeds, grouped splits, adaptive attacks and published nulls.
