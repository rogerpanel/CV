## Q1. Concrete turnaround examples: what changed, and by how much?

### Takeaway
The best-documented turnarounds come from changing the *evaluation* (fixing labels and flow construction, using fair or simpler baselines, adaptive attacks, temporal splits), or from adding *one* targeted module (regularization/tuning, numeric-feature embeddings, adversarial training, conformal rejection). New architectures alone rarely do it. Several famous "wins" turned out to be evaluation artifacts. The credible journal story for SODE-Guard is therefore: "we found X was an artifact, and module Y gives a measured, statistically tested gain."

### Cited Findings
**Security-ML: evaluation changes that changed conclusions**
- Arp et al., "Dos and Don'ts of Machine Learning in Computer Security" (USENIX Security 2022) reviewed 30 papers from top-tier security venues over 10 years and found the pitfalls widespread. They grouped them into data collection/labeling, system design/learning, performance evaluation, and deployment/operation — [USENIX presentation page](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity22/presentation/arp); [seminar summary](https://dnguyencodez.github.io/2024/02/14/Dos-and-Don'ts-of-Machine-Learning-in-Computer-Security-(Seminar-3.2).html)
- Arp et al.'s NIDS case study ran Kitsune (an ensemble of autoencoders) on IoT traffic simulating Mirai activation and propagation. A simple boxplot outlier method on packet frequency in 10-second bins matched Kitsune on ROC AUC and **outperformed it at low false-positive rates**. The authors argued that the Mirai infection in lab data is very conspicuous, which makes it easy to learn spurious correlations. This is the canonical "simple baseline matches the fancy model" case, analogous to the bag-of-words result in the Mamba paper — [arXiv 2010.09470](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.09470); [USENIX PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec22summer_arp.pdf); [seminar summary](https://dnguyencodez.github.io/2024/02/14/Dos-and-Don'ts-of-Machine-Learning-in-Computer-Security-(Seminar-3.2).html)
- Engelen, Rimmer & Joosen, "Troubleshooting an Intrusion Detection Dataset: the CICIDS2017 Case Study" (IEEE SPW/WTMC 2021, DOI 10.1109/SPW53761.2021.00009), found problems in traffic generation, flow construction, feature extraction and labelling. **More than 20% of the original traces were reconstructed or relabelled**, and benchmarks on the corrected data showed significant changes. The fixes in their CICFlowMeter included ending a TCP flow only after FIN packets are exchanged in both directions and treating RST as a flow terminator — [researchportal.be abstract](https://researchportal.be/en/node/8038227); [imec record](https://imec-publications.be/entities/publication/de3203e3-50c3-4fc3-b93e-065b1bf16765); [code mirror](https://gh.r-mj.com/GintsEngelen/WTMC2021-Code); [modified CICFlowMeter](https://www.github.com/lisa-lthorrold/CICFlowMeter)
- Lanvin et al., "Errors in the CICIDS2017 dataset and the significant differences in detection performances it makes" (CRiSIS 2022, LNCS), documented packet misordering, packet duplication, and attacks performed but mislabelled, and measured how the corrections change supervised detection results — [HAL](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-03775466); [IP Paris portal](https://researchportal.ip-paris.fr/en/publications/errors-inthecicids2017-dataset-andthesignificant-differences-inde/)
- Liu et al. (IEEE CNS 2022), "Error Prevalence in NIDS datasets: A Case Study on CIC-IDS-2017 and CSE-CIC-IDS-2018", reported many undocumented errors in attack orchestration, feature generation, documentation and labelling, and released corrected labelling logic — [CPS-VO](https://archive.cps-vo.org/node/97449); [AMiner](https://aminer.org/pub/6389fe5c90e50fcafdcaab09/error-prevalence-in-nids-datasets-a-case-study-on-cic-ids-and)
- arXiv 2401.16843 reports that models can still score well on flawed NIDS data, which hides the underlying problems. In other words, a high score is not evidence that the data is clean — [arXiv 2401.16843](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.16843v1.pdf)
- TESSERACT (Pendlebury et al., USENIX Security 2019) showed that Android malware-classifier F1 scores near 0.99 were inflated by **spatial bias** (train/test class ratios unlike deployment) and **temporal bias** (time-inconsistent splits). It introduced the **AUT** metric, which summarizes robustness to time decay. The original study used 129K apps over about 3 years. The extended version (Kan et al., 2024) used 259,230 samples over 5 years and added Windows PE and PDF case studies, and found that appropriate periodic tuning can recover significant performance — [USENIX page](https://usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity19/presentation/pendlebury); [arXiv 1807.07838](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.07838v4.pdf); [extended arXiv 2402.01359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01359?context=cs); [UCL PDF](https://s2lab.cs.ucl.ac.uk/downloads/tesseract.pdf)
- Transcend → TRANSCENDENT (Barbero et al., IEEE S&P 2022) re-evaluated Transcend, a conformal-evaluation method for flagging drifting malware predictions, on a bias-free 5-year dataset. It added two conformal evaluators (ICE, CCE) that "match or surpass the performance of the original while significantly decreasing the computational overhead" and outperform state-of-the-art rejection approaches. A later survey describes the original thresholds as miscalibrated. This is a direct precedent for "the claim failed re-evaluation, then a conformal module fixed it" — [arXiv 2010.03856](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.03856); [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9833659); [Royal Holloway](https://pure.royalholloway.ac.uk/en/publications/transcending-transcend-revisiting-malware-classification-in-the-p)
- Whisper (Fu et al., CCS 2021) reports that Kitsune achieves only **0.837–0.920 AUC** under five stealthy TCP attacks and cannot detect low-rate scanning. Replacing packet-level features with **frequency-domain features, using the same autoencoder,** improved AUC by up to **15.72%**. The full system improved AUC by up to **18.36%** over state-of-the-art systems across 42 attack types, with roughly 100x higher throughput, and kept about 90% detection accuracy under evasion attacks. This is a feature/context module turnaround rather than an architecture one — [arXiv 2106.14707](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.14707); [BIT record](https://pure.bit.edu.cn/en/publications/realtime-robust-malicious-traffic-detection-via-frequency-domain-/)

**Tabular DL: plain MLPs pulled level with or past GBDTs by tuning and embeddings**
- Kadra, Lindauer, Hutter & Grabocka (NeurIPS 2021), "Well-tuned Simple Nets Excel on Tabular Datasets", tested 40 tabular datasets. For each dataset they jointly searched a "regularization cocktail" over 13 regularizers (e.g., weight decay, dropout, data augmentation, SWA, skip connections). Plain MLPs with the cocktail **statistically significantly outperformed XGBoost, Auto-sklearn's GBDT and CatBoost**. They were significantly better than XGBoost after about a 30-minute tuning budget, and adding early stopping did not help XGBoost — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/c902b497eb972281fb5b4e206db38ee6-Abstract.html); [arXiv 2106.11189](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.11189); [review summary](https://liner.com/review/welltuned-simple-nets-excel-on-tabular-datasets)
- Gorishniy et al. (NeurIPS 2022), "On Embeddings for Numerical Features in Tabular Deep Learning", replaced plain linear numeric embeddings with **piecewise-linear encodings** or **periodic embeddings (PLR)**. With these, DL models "successfully compete with GBDT on GBDT-friendly benchmarks", and simple MLP-like models become comparable to attention-based state-of-the-art models. Periodic embeddings were better on average — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/9e9f0ffc3d836836ca96cbf8fe14b105-Abstract.html); [arXiv 2203.05556](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.05556v4.pdf)

**Robust-ML: defenses broken, and what survived**
- Athalye, Carlini & Wagner (ICML 2018) found that 7 of 8 (7 of 9 in later versions) non-certified ICLR 2018 defenses relied on obfuscated gradients. They circumvented 6 completely and 1 partially. Madry et al.'s **PGD adversarial training** was the only defense that significantly increased robustness, keeping **47% accuracy on CIFAR-10 at L∞ ε = 0.031** — [arXiv 1802.00420](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.00420v2); [code](https://github.com/anishathalye/obfuscated-gradients); [ICML slides](https://nicholas.carlini.com/slides/2018_icml_obfuscatedgradients.pdf)

### Inferences
- SODE-Guard's ICLR failure matches the robust-ML pattern: the adversarially trained MLP was best. That is the Athalye et al. lesson that adversarial training is the baseline that survives. The journal version should **add adversarial training to SODE-Guard** (an SDE + AT variant) rather than claim intrinsic SDE robustness. A fair claim is "SDE noise is complementary to AT" only if AT-SDE beats AT-MLP with paired tests.
- The Kadra and Gorishniy results imply that the 0.43 clean macro-F1 may partly reflect **under-tuning and raw numeric inputs**. Two cheap, citable modules to try before any architectural change are periodic/PLR numeric embeddings for flow features and a tuned regularization cocktail. GBDTs (XGBoost/LightGBM/CatBoost) must be in the baseline set. Reviewers will ask for them on tabular flow data.
- The Engelen, Lanvin and Liu results imply that a ranking change can come from data hygiene alone. A data audit of ICS3D (flow termination, duplicate flows, label timing windows, constant or shortcut features) is a legitimate "evaluation change" section in its own right.
- [prior knowledge, verify] Other turnaround and counter-evidence papers worth citing: Gorishniy et al. 2021, "Revisiting Deep Learning Models for Tabular Data" (ResNet/FT-Transformer; no universal winner versus GBDT); Grinsztajn et al. 2022, "Why do tree-based models still outperform deep learning on tabular data?" (trees still win on medium-sized data, a counterweight); Tramèr et al. 2020, "On Adaptive Attacks to Adversarial Example Defenses" (13 defenses circumvented); Croce & Hein 2020, AutoAttack (reduced reported robustness for many published defenses); Jacobs et al., CCS 2022, "AI/ML for Network Security: The Emperor has no Clothes" (Trustee; shortcut features in NIDS datasets); Flood et al., EuroS&P 2024, "Bad Design Smells in Benchmark NIDS Datasets" (my search did not confirm it).

## Q2. Modules analogous to "chunk-level scoring" for network flows (MIL, session/window aggregation, temporal context, flow-sequence/graph models)

### Takeaway
The flow-level analogue of chunk-level MIL scoring is **bagging flows per host, container or pod and time window** (or per source–destination pair) and scoring the bag. Graph-context models (E-GraphSAGE) and temporal or frequency aggregation (Whisper, Tracegram) are the published variants. Gains are well documented qualitatively. Clean macro-F1 deltas on multi-class imbalanced data are scarce, so SODE-Guard would be reporting a new measurement, not repeating one.

### Cited Findings
- MIL for network traffic: a discriminative multi-instance model treats each computer's traffic in a **fixed 5-minute window as a bag of flows**. Only bag-level labels ("presumed infected vs. presumed clean") are needed — [arXiv 1703.02868](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.02868); [Franc et al., ECML 2015, "Learning detector of malicious network traffic"](https://msofka.github.io/pdfs/franc-ecml15.pdf)
- Cisco/Talos MIL detectors build bags per user (or source IP) and hostname over a fixed period, containing all communication with that hostname. A related Cisco patent groups flows that share host name, target domain and time window into one bag — [Talos blog](https://blog.talosintelligence.com/cognitive-research-learning-detectors/); [US Patent 11,271,833](https://uspto.report/patent/grant/11,271,833)
- Other bag definitions group flows by source–destination address, where a bag may contain both malicious and benign flows (encrypted-traffic MIL, ICCS 2025) — [ICCS 2025 paper](https://www.iccs-meeting.org/archive/iccs2025/papers/159040260.pdf)
- Tracegram (USENIX Security 2026, prepub) uses MIL at trace level, combining per-flow encoders with a **temporally aware aggregation module** across flows — [USENIX Sec '26 prepub](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity26/sec26_prepub_qu.pdf)
- A 2024 NIDS paper uses a per-host time-window aggregation of flow data and reports it as faster and more transferable than graph-based methods — [arXiv 2410.18658](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18658v2)
- E-GraphSAGE (Lo et al.) builds a graph with hosts as nodes and flows as edge features and classifies edges. Reported binary F1: **1.00 on BoT-IoT, 0.99 on ToN-IoT, 0.97 on NF-BoT-IoT, 1.00 on NF-ToN-IoT**. It beats the best prior classifiers on ToN-IoT and BoT-IoT and only matches state-of-the-art on the NetFlow variants. One snippet cites about 97% multiclass F1 on BoT-IoT. A follow-up, EG-ConMix, adds contrastive learning and augmentation and reports better macro-F1 and robustness across partitions — [arXiv 2103.16329](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.16329); [EG-ConMix review](https://www.themoonlight.io/fr/review/eg-conmix-an-intrusion-detection-method-based-on-graph-contrastive-learning)
- Whisper aggregates per-flow packet sequences into **frequency-domain features**, a temporal-context module, giving up to +15.72% AUC with the same autoencoder (see Q1) — [arXiv 2106.14707](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.14707)

### Inferences
- For ICS3D container flows, the most direct analogue of the +0.20–0.38 macro-F1 chunk-scoring gain is: (i) bag = all flows of a container or pod (or a src–dst service pair) in a sliding window of W seconds; (ii) per-flow encoder (the SDE, or an MLP baseline); (iii) attention-MIL or noisy-OR pooling to a bag score; (iv) report both flow-level and window-level macro-F1. Flow-level labels can be recovered from attention weights. Window-level evaluation has to be **declared and justified operationally**, since alerting per container per window is what a SOC acts on. Otherwise reviewers will see it as changing the metric to win.
- The SDE's natural strength is continuous-time dynamics, so modelling a *sequence of flows per container* as an irregularly sampled time series is a better fit than per-flow tabular classification. That may be the honest place for a neural-SDE advantage to show up. It must be compared against a GRU or Transformer on the same flow sequences and a GBDT on window-aggregated statistics. The window-GBDT is the "bag-of-words" equivalent, and it may match the SDE.
- Grouped splits are mandatory with bagging: windows from the same container or attack episode must not straddle train and test.
- [prior knowledge, verify] Other relevant context models to cite as baselines or related work: nPrint (Holland et al., CCS 2021, standard packet representation plus AutoML); ET-BERT and YaTC (pre-trained traffic transformers); netFound; the NetFlow-standardized NF-* datasets from Sarhan et al., which also support the E-GraphSAGE cross-dataset protocol.

### Gaps
- I found no paper reporting **macro-F1 before/after adding MIL or window aggregation on a multi-class imbalanced NIDS dataset** with the same base encoder. The size of a flow-level "chunk scoring" gain is therefore unknown and would be a novel measurement.
- Tracegram's numbers were not retrievable (the USENIX PDF was blocked).

## Q3. Calibration, conformal and ACI alarm modules, and open-set/novelty detection for NIDS

### Takeaway
Split-conformal thresholding with a bounded false-alarm rate is now a recognized NIDS module (2025–2026 papers on O-RAN, UAV and streaming IDS). Conformal *evaluation and rejection* for drift has a strong security precedent (Transcend/TRANSCENDENT). The guarantees are marginal and need exchangeability. Streaming or drifted traffic breaks that assumption, which is exactly the argument for ACI or rolling calibration, and reviewers will expect it to be stated and empirically checked.

### Cited Findings
- O-RAN intrusion monitoring (ICML 2026): a covariance-aware conformal detector uses the **Mahalanobis distance in autoencoder reconstruction-error space** as the nonconformity score, with split-conformal thresholds for a target false-alarm rate (FAR). It reports a higher detection rate than a conformal baseline at the same FAR — [ICML 2026 listing](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/78428)
- CALIBURN (2026): a Conformal Risk Control wrapper turns an **alert budget α** into a threshold with marginal false-positive validity under exchangeability, for streaming NIDS — [alphaXiv 2605.24696](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.24696.md)
- FIRCE (2026): uses conformal p-values for **drift detection** with a fixed-size rolling calibration buffer, initialized from training data and updated with recent samples (IoT) — [alphaXiv 2605.01962](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.01962); [emergentmind](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2605.01962)
- HADAR-UAV: conformal threshold calibration with a guarantee that FAR ≤ a user-specified α with high probability, described as the first conformal UAV IDS — [Tech Science CMC](https://www.techscience.com/cmc/v89n1/68370/html)
- Botnet detection with a conformal rejection layer (arXiv 2409.00667): rejects **58.20% of incorrect predictions on ISCX and 98.94% on ISOT**, alongside adversarial-attack mitigation — [arXiv 2409.00667](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.00667)
- TRANSCENDENT (S&P 2022): conformal evaluators (ICE/CCE) for rejecting drifting predictions in malware detection (see Q1) — [arXiv 2010.03856](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.03856)
- Open-set NIDS: the multi-class Energy-based Flow Classifier (Souza et al.), evaluated on CICIDS2017, "excelled in identifying unknown attacks in open-set datasets" compared with closed-set multi-class classifiers. Its "energy" is a statistical-physics (Potts-model) energy, not the logit energy score from the OOD literature — [arXiv 2109.11224](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.11224)
- In CITADEL (IIoT jamming, not flow NIDS), the logit energy score (negative log-sum-exp) and penultimate-layer Mahalanobis distance are combined with equal weights, and thresholds are set by **blocked temporal cross-validation**, a design that transfers to flow data — [arXiv 2606.22939](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22939)

### Inferences
- A defensible SODE-Guard alarm stack: Platt or temperature scaling (report ECE and Brier), then split-conformal thresholding on benign calibration windows for a target FAR (e.g., 1%), then ACI (Gibbs & Candès 2021 [prior knowledge, verify]) or a rolling calibration buffer in temporal order. Report **empirical FAR versus target α over time**, not just a coverage average. A neural SDE's predictive variance can serve as one nonconformity score, a plausible honest niche for the SDE. It should be compared against MSP, energy and Mahalanobis scores computed from the MLP and GBDT.
- For unseen classes, use a leave-one-attack-family-out protocol on ICS3D's 9 classes and report AUROC, and TPR at 1% FPR, for MSP, energy, Mahalanobis and SDE variance.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that benchmarks energy, Mahalanobis and MSP head-to-head on flow NIDS with leave-one-attack-out holdouts.
- I found no NIDS paper that applies **ACI specifically** and reports FAR tracking under drift. CALIBURN and FIRCE are the closest, and their exact FAR numbers were not retrieved.

## Q4. Statistical and evaluation standards that TIFS/TDSC/Computers & Security reviewers expect (robustness and NIDS)

### Takeaway
Reviewers apply the Arp et al. pitfall list, TESSERACT-style temporal and spatial realism, and the Carlini et al. adaptive-attack checklist. A comeback paper is credible when it shows multiple datasets, strong and simple baselines (GBDT, tuned MLP, AT-MLP, randomized smoothing), adaptive white-box attacks against the full pipeline (including the stochastic SDE, with EOT), multiple seeds with paired tests and multiplicity correction, and released code with corrected data.

### Cited Findings
- Arp et al.'s pitfalls cover data collection/labeling, system design/learning, performance evaluation, and deployment/operation, and were common across 30 top-venue papers. The NIDS case study shows that a simple baseline is needed to expose artifacts in lab data — [arXiv 2010.09470](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.09470); [seminar summary](https://dnguyencodez.github.io/2024/02/14/Dos-and-Don'ts-of-Machine-Learning-in-Computer-Security-(Seminar-3.2).html)
- TESSERACT: enforce temporal consistency (train strictly before test), realistic class ratios in test data (spatial), and report AUT across test periods — [USENIX page](https://usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity19/presentation/pendlebury); [arXiv 1807.07838](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.07838v4.pdf)
- Carlini et al. 2019, "On Evaluating Adversarial Robustness": most defenses are quickly broken by **adaptive attacks**. Evaluate against an adaptive adversary under a stated threat model, check for gradient masking, report clean accuracy alongside robust accuracy, and run sanity checks (iterative attacks beat single-step attacks; success rises with budget; unbounded attacks reach 100%). It is maintained as a living document — [arXiv 1902.06705](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06705v1); [deepai summary](https://deepai.org/publication/on-evaluating-adversarial-robustness)
- Athalye et al. 2018: stochastic or randomized defenses can obfuscate gradients and need specific attacks. This applies directly to an SDE's injected noise, which calls for Expectation-over-Transformation attacks — [arXiv 1802.00420](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.00420v2)
- NIDS data-quality literature (Engelen 2021; Lanvin 2022; Liu 2022) expects authors to use **corrected dataset versions** and state which version they used — [researchportal.be](https://researchportal.be/en/node/8038227); [HAL](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-03775466); [CPS-VO](https://archive.cps-vo.org/node/97449)

### Inferences
- Concrete checklist for the resubmission, mirroring the internal Mamba paper:
  - ≥5 seeds, with mean ± 95% CI.
  - Paired bootstrap (≥1,000 resamples, resampling grouped units such as containers or episodes, not individual flows) for every model-versus-baseline comparison.
  - Holm–Bonferroni across the comparison family.
  - Grouped splits by container, host or attack episode.
  - Temporal splits (train on early days, test on later ones) with an AUT-style decay curve.
  - Leave-one-attack-family-out for novelty.
  - Macro-F1 *and* per-class F1, PR-AUC, TPR at FPR ∈ {0.1%, 1%}.
  - Calibration metrics (ECE, Brier).
  - Conformal FAR-versus-α plots.
  - Adaptive attacks: PGD/APGD with EOT through the SDE solver, plus feature-space constraints for realistic flows (bounded, integer and monotone features).
  - Certification comparison against randomized smoothing at matched noise.
  - Clean-versus-robust trade-off tables.
  - Released code, configs and the corrected data pipeline.
- Honest null ablations, as in the LLM-triage example, should be reported in the main text. Journals such as Computers & Security and TDSC accept "module X did not help" when it is paired with a module that did, with tests.
- [prior knowledge, verify] Additional references reviewers commonly invoke: Sommer & Paxson 2010, "Outside the Closed World" (S&P); Apruzzese et al., "SoK: Pragmatic Assessment of Machine Learning for Network Intrusion Detection" (EuroS&P 2023); Apruzzese et al., "Modeling Realistic Adversarial Attacks against Network Intrusion Detection Systems" (DTRAP 2022), on feasible feature-space attacks; Croce & Hein 2020, AutoAttack; Tramèr et al. 2020, adaptive attacks; Cohen et al. 2019, randomized smoothing; Demšar 2006 (critical-difference tests across datasets).

### Gaps
- No public, explicit reviewer guidelines specific to IEEE TIFS, TDSC or Computers & Security for ML-NIDS evaluation were found. The expectations above are inferred from the community's methodological papers.

## Q5. Public container/cloud/Kubernetes intrusion datasets for a second-dataset evaluation

### Takeaway
Public *container-specific* flow datasets are few. The METU Kubernetes misuse dataset is the strongest direct match. For a credible second and third dataset, pair it with a standard flow benchmark that has corrected labels (Engelen's CICIDS2017 fix, or a NetFlow-standardized NF-* set).

### Cited Findings
- METU "Kubernetes dataset for misuse detection" (Sever & Doğan, ITU Journal on Future and Evolving Technologies, vol. 4 issue 2, 2023): network flows from a Kubernetes cluster running microservices, with **10 attack scenarios** drawn from CVEs with distinct CWE classes plus benign flows. Code and data are on GitHub (kubernetes-dataset) — [ITU PDF](https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-s/opb/jnl/S-JNL-VOL4.ISSUE2-2023-A26-PDF-E.pdf); [ITU record](https://itu.int/pub/S-JNL-VOL4.ISSUE2-2023-A26); [METU AVESIS](https://avesis.metu.edu.tr/yayin/faf7d288-1b05-420b-9559-deea962383af/a-kubernetes-dataset-for-misuse-detection); [Aperta record (2024)](https://aperta.ulakbim.gov.tr/records/273835)
- METU MSc thesis (Oğur, 2022): container-attack dataset built from vulnerable container images deployed under Kubernetes, based on CVE listings — [METU thesis](https://open.metu.edu.tr/handle/11511/97882)
- Kubernetes testbed (arXiv 2410.18332, 2024): Docker plus Kubernetes with Weave Net, eBPF/XDP capture, DoS/DDoS attacks (e.g., TCP SYN floods), aimed at ML IDS — [arXiv 2410.18332](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2410.18332)
- A cloud-server attack dataset covering 22 common attacks (IJCNC, AWS-oriented; I could not confirm a container-orchestration focus) — [IJCNC](https://aircconline.com/ijcnc/V18N2/18226cnc06.pdf)
- E-GraphSAGE's evaluation shows the common cross-dataset set: BoT-IoT, ToN-IoT, NF-BoT-IoT, NF-ToN-IoT — [arXiv 2103.16329](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.16329)
- Corrected CICIDS2017 (Engelen et al.) with released tooling — [researchportal.be](https://researchportal.be/en/node/8038227); [code mirror](https://gh.r-mj.com/GintsEngelen/WTMC2021-Code)

### Inferences
- Recommended evaluation set: ICS3D (primary), METU Kubernetes (second container dataset, which also allows a cross-dataset transfer test), and corrected CICIDS2017 or an NF-* NetFlow set (a widely recognized benchmark). With ≥3 datasets, use a Friedman/Nemenyi critical-difference test or report per-dataset Holm-corrected paired bootstraps.
- Feature-schema harmonization (for example, NetFlow-v9-style fields across all datasets) allows a train-on-A / test-on-B generalization experiment, which is a strong signal of reviewer-facing rigour.

### Gaps
- My search did not find any public record under the name "ICS3D". arXiv 2509.23305 appeared in the results and may be related, but I could not open it. The user should confirm ICS3D's provenance and citation.
- I found no public Kubernetes **runtime or syscall** (host-telemetry) intrusion dataset in this search. I did not check dataset licences.
