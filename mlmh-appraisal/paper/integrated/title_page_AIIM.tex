\textbf{Abstract.} \textbf{Background and objective.} The open actigraphy cohorts DEPRESJON (depression), PSYKOSE (schizophrenia) and HYPERAKTIV (ADHD), harmonised as OBF-Psychiatric, are the most used public benchmarks for machine-learning detection of mental disorders from wearables, and reported accuracies have risen from the dataset authors' leave-one-patient-out 0.7 to above 0.9. We asked whether this reflects better models or weaker evaluation, and measured what the prevalent practices cost. \textbf{Methods.} Part~I appraises every published supervised model on the four cohorts with PROBAST+AI and TRIPOD+AI, recording the unit of train/test splitting, external validation, calibration reporting, events per predictor, code availability and handling of the control group shared by DEPRESJON and PSYKOSE. Part~II re-evaluates the cohorts with models fixed a priori, ten seeds and participant-level bootstrap intervals: record-wise versus subject-wise cross-validation (E1), frozen cross-cohort transfer (E2), calibration and recalibration (E3), sex strata (E4), transdiagnostic and five-class tasks (E5), and ablation (E6). \textbf{Results.} Of 28 published models, 7 split by participant, 9 by day and 12 did not state the unit; none reported calibration or validated on a second cohort. Record-wise splitting inflated AUROC by 0.065 to 0.114 (DEPRESJON), 0.020 to 0.044 (PSYKOSE) and 0.130 to 0.169 (HYPERAKTIV), where the honest estimate was at chance. Models trained on PSYKOSE lost 0.13 to 0.21 AUROC on DEPRESJON with calibration slopes of 0.27 to 0.63; honest slopes were 0.53 to 0.95, and recalibration from the training cohort did not repair the transfer. Honest performance was stable across feature subsets and cross-validation schemes. \textbf{Conclusions.} Reported progress on these benchmarks is largely an artefact of evaluation design. We provide subject-wise, calibrated, cross-cohort baselines with public code and manifests against which future claims can be checked.
